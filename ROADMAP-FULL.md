# Overview: Your English Journey

---

## Welcome

You are holding a complete path from **no English** to **strong, confident English**. This roadmap was written for **young women in Afghanistan** who want to learn **American English** through **self-study** — often at home, often on a phone, often without a teacher.

If you can read this, you are already learning.

> **Remember:** A broken schedule is not a broken dream. You can restart any day.

---

## Who this roadmap serves

- **Age:** About 18–25 (young adult topics and respect)
- **First language:** Dari
- **Time:** About 30–45 minutes per day
- **Device:** Smartphone
- **English type:** American English
- **Mode:** Self-study (no teacher required)

---

## What “mastery” means here

Mastery does not mean perfection. It means you can:

- Understand everyday American speech at normal speed
- Express your ideas clearly in speech and writing
- Learn new English on your own (videos, articles, courses)
- Use English for **your goals** — study, work online, help family, connect with the world

That is a realistic, powerful kind of mastery.

---

## Five stages (0 → 4)

| Stage | Name | Time (30–45 min/day) | You can… |
|-------|------|----------------------|----------|
| **0** | Pre-beginner | Weeks 1–2 | Read the alphabet, say hello, use 50 survival words |
| **1** | Beginner (A1–A2) | Months 1–3 | Talk about daily life in present tense, write short messages |
| **2** | Intermediate (B1) | Months 4–8 | Tell stories in past tense, share opinions, write paragraphs |
| **3** | Upper intermediate (B2–C1) | Months 9–14 | Discuss abstract topics, read longer texts, write formal emails |
| **4** | Advanced (C1–C2) | Months 15–24+ | Use nuance, persuade, present, write professionally |

**Honest truth:** Life in difficult circumstances may slow you down. The roadmap still works if you study **3 days a week** — just extend the calendar.

## Safety and privacy

### Study space

- Choose a place where you feel **safe and focused** (home corner, quiet room).
- Use **headphones** when listening so others are not disturbed.
- If someone questions your study, you may say simply: *“I am learning a skill for my future.”* You do not owe a long explanation.

### Digital safety

- Use **strong passwords** on learning accounts.
- Be careful with **voice recordings** — store in a private folder; delete old files.
- Do not share personal location or contact details in language exchange apps.
- Prefer **well-known free apps** listed in **[15-resources.md](15-resources.md)**.

### Emotional safety

- Compare yourself to **your past self**, not to influencers or fluent speakers online.
- Shame slows learning. **Curiosity speeds it up.**
- Rest days are allowed. A rest day is not quitting.

## Dari speaker advantage

You already speak a rich language. English will feel strange at first — **that is normal**.

Common differences (you will learn fixes in modules):

| Dari | English |
|------|---------|
| No articles (*a*, *the*) | Articles required |
| Verb often at end | Subject–verb–object order |
| Different vowel sounds | New vowel pairs (ship/sheep) |
| Persian script vs Latin alphabet | New writing system at start |

Your brain can learn both. Millions of Dari speakers have learned English — you are on the same path.

Every house needs a floor. Alphabet and survival English are your floor. You can greet someone, read simple signs, and start grammar without fear.

> *Day 1 win: spell your name in English letters.*

---

## Level focus

**Stage 0** (pre-beginner) and early **Stage 1**.

---

## Step-by-step lessons

### Lesson 1 — The alphabet (20 min)

English has **26 letters**. Names are capitalized: **Fatima**, **Kabul**.

**Vowels:** A E I O U (sometimes Y)

**Consonants:** B C D F G H J K L M N P Q R S T V W X Y Z

**Practice:** Say each letter aloud. Write: A a B b C c …

**Phone tip:** Switch keyboard to **English (US)**. Long-press globe icon.

> **Dari note:** English letters look different from Arabic script. Your eyes will adapt in 1–2 weeks with daily practice.

---

### Lesson 2 — Sounds that match letters (15 min)

Most letters have one common sound:

| Letter | Example word |
|--------|--------------|
| B | **b**ook |
| F | **f**ood |
| M | **m**other |

Some are special (learn more in [05-pronunciation.md](05-pronunciation.md)):

- **Th** — *thank*, *this*
- **Sh** — *she*
- **Ch** — *child*

---

### Lesson 3 — First 50 words (20 min)

Learn in **groups of 10** per day.

**Group 1 — People**

| English | Meaning idea |
|---------|----------------|
| I | I |
| you | you |
| he | he |
| she | she |
| we | we |
| they | they |
| man | man |
| woman | woman |
| child | child |
| family | family |

**Group 2 — Home**

house, room, door, water, food, bread, tea, day, night, home

**Group 3 — Actions**

go, come, eat, drink, read, write, see, hear, want, need

**Group 4 — Good / bad**

good, bad, big, small, hot, cold, happy, sad, yes, no

**Group 5 — Connectors**

and, but, in, on, here, there, now, today, please, thank you

---

### Lesson 4 — Numbers and time (15 min)

**1–20:** one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, ten, eleven, twelve, thirteen, fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, seventeen, eighteen, nineteen, twenty

**Useful phrases:**

- What time is it?
- today / tomorrow / yesterday
- morning / afternoon / evening

---

### Lesson 5 — Survival dialogues (20 min)

**Dialogue A — Meeting**

- **A:** Hello. My name is Sara.  
- **B:** Hi, Sara. I am Mary. Nice to meet you.  
- **A:** Nice to meet you too.

**Dialogue B — Thanks**

- **A:** Thank you for your help.  
- **B:** You are welcome.

**Dialogue C — Help**

- Excuse me. I need help, please.  
- I do not understand. Can you speak slowly?

**Dialogue D — Health (simple)**

- I am not well today.  
- I need water, please.

Practice: Read aloud 3 times. Record on phone. Listen once.

---

### Lesson 6 — Second 50 words (20 min)

study, learn, English, book, phone, internet, message, friend, sister, work, school, doctor, health, problem, question, answer, open, close, wait, stop, start, look, listen, speak, say, tell, know, think, like, love, live, city, country, world, name, age, year, month, week, hour, minute, money, free, new, old, right, left, up, down

# Core Grammar

**Stage 1** · Months 1–3 (with foundations)

---

## Learning goals

1. Use **verb be** (am, is, are) correctly
2. Use **have / has** for possession and facts
3. Build **present simple** sentences (habits and facts)
4. Ask **yes/no** and **wh-** questions
5. Use **negatives** (don't, doesn't, not)
6. Use **there is / there are**

---

## Why this skill matters

Grammar is the skeleton of English. With these patterns, you can describe your life, ask questions, and stop guessing word order.

> *Learn patterns, not single sentences. Swap one word — make a new sentence.*

---

## Level focus

**Stage 1** (A1–A2).

---

## Step-by-step lessons

### Lesson 1 — Subject + be (20 min)

| Subject | Be | Example |
|---------|-----|---------|
| I | am | I am tired. |
| You | you are | You are kind. |
| He / she / it | is | She is at home. |
| We / they | are | We are students. |

**Negative:** add **not**

- I am **not** busy.
- She is **not** here.

**Questions:** swap order

- **Are** you fine? → Yes, I am. / No, I am not.
- **Is** he home?

> **Dari note:** Dari often drops “to be” in present. In English, **do not drop it**: say *I am fine*, not *I fine*.

---

### Lesson 2 — Have / has (20 min)

Use **have** and **has** to show **possession** (I have a book) or some **states** (I have time / She has a problem).

| Subject | Form | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| I / you / we / they | **have** | I have a phone. |
| He / she / it | **has** | She has a sister. |

**Negative:**

- I **do not have** (don't have) Wi-Fi today.
- He **does not have** (doesn't have) class on Friday.

**Questions:**

- **Do** you have a quiet room? → Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
- **Does** she have your number? → Yes, she does.

**Contrast with *be*:**

- I **am** tired. (state — adjective)
- I **have** time. (possession / availability)
- She **is** a student. (identity)
- She **has** a book. (possession)

> **Dari note:** داشتن (*dâštan* — to have) works differently. English uses **have/has** in present, not a separate verb like *am/is*.

**Pattern practice:**

- I have + noun → I have a notebook.
- She has + noun → She has courage.
- Do you have + noun? → Do you have internet?

---

### Lesson 3 — Present simple (20 min)

Use for **habits** and **facts**.

| Subject | Verb | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| I / you / we / they | study | I study English. |
| He / she / it | studies (+s) | She **studies** at home. |

**Negative:**

- I **do not** (don't) study at night.
- He **does not** (doesn't) study at night.

**Questions:**

- **Do** you study English? → Yes, I do.
- **Does** she study English? → Yes, she does.

---

### Lesson 4 — Wh- questions (15 min)

| Word | Asks about |
|------|------------|
| What | thing |
| Where | place |
| When | time |
| Who | person |
| Why | reason |
| How | way |

- **What** do you study? → I study English.
- **Where** do you live?
- **How** do you learn? → I learn on my phone.

---

### Lesson 5 — There is / there are (15 min)

| Use | Example |
|-----|---------|
| There is + singular | There is a book on the table. |
| There are + plural | There are two windows. |

**Negative:** There is **not** / There are **not** (no)

**Question:** **Is** there a phone here?

---

### Lesson 6 — Articles a / an / the (20 min)

| Article | Use |
|---------|-----|
| a | one general thing: a book |
| an | before vowel sound: an apple |
| the | specific thing: the book (we know which one) |

**Examples:**

- I need **a** pen.
- She reads **an** article.
- **The** internet is slow today.

> **Dari note:** Dari has no *a/the*. Practice **out loud** with every new noun.

---

### Lesson 7 — Object pronouns and possessives (15 min)

| Subject | Object | Possessive |
|---------|--------|------------|
| I | me | my |
| you | you | your |
| he | him | his |
| she | her | her |
| we | us | our |
| they | them | their |

- I help **her**. **Her** book is new.
- **Our** family is kind.

---

## Pattern practice (swap words)

**Pattern:** I + verb + noun

- I study English.
- I read books.
- I drink tea.

**Pattern:** She + verb-s + noun

- She works at home.
- She learns online.

---

## Examples

**Daily routine:**

> I wake up at six. I have tea and bread. I study English for thirty minutes. My sister has a phone; she works at home. We have lunch together. In the evening, I read messages on my phone. I do not have much free time, but I have hope.

**Dialogue — At home:**

> **A:** Do you have a quiet room for study?  
> **B:** Yes, I have a small room. I do not have a desk, but I have a chair and my phone.  
> **A:** Does your sister have English books?  
> **B:** She has one old book. We have the internet on good days.

**Questions for practice:**

> What do you do in the morning? Where does your family live? Is there a quiet room for study?

# Vocabulary Building

**Stage 1 → Stage 2** · Ongoing daily habit

---

## Learning goals

1. Learn **high-frequency words** in themed groups
2. Use **word families** (act → action → active)
3. Apply **spaced repetition** without paid apps
4. Reach **300 words** (month 2) and **800+** (month 6)
5. Connect new words to **speaking and writing** same day

---

## Why this skill matters

Grammar without words is an empty house. Vocabulary lets you say what you **feel** and **need**.

> *Ten new words today, used in one sentence each, beat fifty words memorized silently.*

---

## Level focus

**Stage 1–3** (never stop learning words).

---

## Step-by-step lessons

### Lesson 1 — How to learn a new word (15 min)

For each word, save **4 parts**:

| Part | Example: *patient* |
|------|---------------------|
| English | patient |
| Meaning (simple) | able to wait calmly |
| Example sentence | I am patient with my English. |
| Pronunciation note | PAY-shunt (stress first syllable) |

**Same day:** speak the sentence aloud.

---

### Lesson 2 — Top 50 verbs (20 min)

be, have, do, say, get, make, go, know, take, see, come, think, look, want, give, use, find, tell, ask, work, seem, feel, try, leave, call, need, become, keep, let, begin, help, show, hear, play, run, move, live, believe, bring, happen, write, sit, stand, lose, pay, meet, include, continue, set, learn, change

---

### Lesson 3 — Top 50 nouns for your life (20 min)

time, year, people, way, day, woman, man, child, world, life, hand, part, place, work, week, case, point, government, company, number, night, home, water, room, mother, father, area, money, story, fact, month, lot, right, study, book, eye, job, word, business, issue, side, kind, head, house, service, friend, father, power, hour, game, line, end, member, health, idea, food, student, teacher, language, phone, internet, message, problem, answer, question

---

### Lesson 4 — Thematic sets (learn 10/week)

**Home & daily life:** kitchen, wash, clean, cook, sleep, wake, tired, busy, quiet, safe

**Health:** doctor, medicine, pain, better, rest, exercise, body, heart, sick, well

**Technology:** download, app, Wi-Fi, password, screen, charge, battery, click, search, online

**Emotions & strength:** hope, courage, proud, worry, calm, strong, free, dream, choose, try again

---

### Lesson 5 — Word families (15 min)

| Root | Family |
|------|--------|
| study | student, studied, studying |
| help | helpful, helper, helpless (careful: negative meaning) |
| create | creative, creation, creator |
| educate | education, educational, educator |

**Rule:** Learn the verb first, then nouns/adjectives.

---

### Lesson 6 — Spaced repetition (phone method) (15 min)

**Day 1:** Learn 10 words  
**Day 2:** Review Day 1 + learn 10 new  
**Day 4:** Review all  
**Day 7:** Review all  
**Day 14:** Review all  

**Tool:** Notes app — title `English words Week 3`. Move mastered words to `DONE` section.

| 2 | 300 |
| 4 | 500 |
| 6 | 800 |
| 12 | 1200+ |

“Active” = you can use it in a sentence, not only recognize it.

# Pronunciation (American English)

**Stage 1 → Stage 4** · Daily 5–10 min alongside other skills

---

## Learning goals

1. Hear and produce **American vowels** (ship vs sheep)
2. Pronounce **th**, **r**, and **w** more clearly
3. Use **word stress** so listeners understand you
4. Fix **Dari-influenced** sound habits
5. Use your phone to **record and compare**

---

## Why this skill matters

Good grammar with unclear sounds still blocks communication. Clear pronunciation builds **confidence** — people listen to your ideas, not only your accent.

> *Goal: understood, not perfect. Native-like accent is optional.*

---

## Level focus

**Stage 1** starts; refine through **Stage 4**.

---

## Step-by-step lessons

### Lesson 1 — American English basics (15 min)

- **R** is stronger than in British English: *water*, *far*
- **T** between vowels sounds like quick *d* in casual speech: *water*, *better* (later stage)
- Final consonants: try to **finish** the sound: *help*, not *hel*

---

### Lesson 2 — Vowel pairs (20 min)

| Sound | Example | Common mistake |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| /ɪ/ short i | **sh**i**p** | sounds like sheep |
| /iː/ long ee | sh**ee**p | |
| /ɛ/ | b**e**d | |
| /æ/ | c**a**t | |
| /ʌ/ | c**u**p | |
| /ɑː/ | f**a**ther | |

**Practice sentence:**  
*I sit in a big city and drink tea.*

Say slowly. Record. Listen.

---

### Lesson 3 — Consonants for Dari speakers (20 min)

**TH (voiceless)** — *thank, think, three*  
- Tongue lightly between teeth, air out  
- Not *tank* or *sank*

**TH (voiced)** — *this, that, mother*  
- Tongue between teeth, voice on

**P vs F** — *pat* vs *fat* — lips vs teeth

**V vs W** — *very* vs *west* — teeth on lip for *v*

**H** — *he, home* — breathe out, not silent

> **Dari note:** Sounds that do not exist in Dari need **daily 2-minute drills**. Short daily beats long weekly.

---

### Lesson 4 — Word stress (15 min)

English stressed syllable is **louder and longer**.

| Word | Stress (CAPS) |
|------|---------------|
| PHOtograph | pho-TO-graphy (shift!) |
| comPUter | |
| eduCAtion | |
| paTIENT | |

**Rule:** Stress wrong syllable → word sounds foreign or unclear.

**Practice:** Clap on stressed syllable: com-**PU**-ter

---

### Lesson 5 — Sentence rhythm (15 min)

Content words (nouns, verbs) stronger; function words (*a, the, to*) softer.

- I **want** to **study** **English**.
- She **lives** at **home**.

Read aloud with exaggeration first, then natural speed.

---

### Lesson 6 — Phone recording routine (10 min daily)

1. Read 3 sentences from any module  
2. Record voice memo  
3. Listen — note one sound to fix tomorrow  
4. Optional: compare to slow American audio (see [15-resources.md](15-resources.md))  
5. **Privacy:** store in locked/private folder; delete monthly

---

## Examples

**Minimal pairs (train your ear):**

| A | B |
|---|---|
| ship | sheep |
| live (verb) | leave |
| very | berry (different vowels) |

**Introduction with clear stress:**

> **HEL**-lo. **MY** **NAME** **IS** Zahra. I **STUD**-y **ENG**-lish at **HOME**.

Then make it natural without losing key stresses.

# Listening Skills

**Stage 1 → Stage 4** · 10–15 min daily recommended

---

## Learning goals

1. Understand **slow, clear** English (Stage 1)
2. Move to **natural speed** short clips (Stage 2+)
3. Use **dictation** to train your ear
4. **Wean off subtitles** gradually
5. Keep learning when **internet fails** (downloaded audio)

---

## Why this skill matters

Real English is heard before it is read. Listening trains your brain for **speed, rhythm, and real grammar** — not only textbook English.

> *If you understand 60% of a clip, it is the right level. Not 95% — too easy. Not 20% — too hard.*

---

## Level focus

**Stage 1:** slow · **Stage 2:** normal short · **Stage 3+:** podcasts, lectures

---

## Step-by-step lessons

### Lesson 1 — Listening levels (10 min)

| Level | What you hear | Goal |
|-------|---------------|------|
| 1 | Single words, slow | Recognize sounds |
| 2 | Short sentences, slow | Catch subject + verb |
| 3 | 1–2 min dialogue, clear | Main idea |
| 4 | News/podcast clips | Details + opinion |
| 5 | Movies, fast speech | Slang, humor (advanced) |

Move up when you understand **main idea** without pausing every line.

---

### Lesson 2 — Daily listening routine (15 min)

| Step | Minutes | Action |
|------|---------|--------|
| 1 | 2 | Predict topic from title |
| 2 | 5 | Listen once — no text |
| 3 | 5 | Listen again — note 3 words you heard |
| 4 | 3 | Listen with transcript — check |
| 5 | optional | Shadow last sentence (repeat aloud) |

---

### Lesson 3 — Dictation (15 min)

1. Play **one sentence** (slow English)
2. Pause
3. Write what you hear
4. Check transcript
5. Fix spelling and missed words

**Beginner sentence example:**  
*“I study English at home every day.”*

---

### Lesson 4 — Subtitle strategy (10 min)

| Phase | Subtitles |
|-------|-----------|
| Week 1–4 | English subtitles ON |
| Month 2–3 | English subtitles, pause less |
| Month 4+ | Try without subtitles, then check |
| Advanced | No subtitles first |

**Avoid:** Dari subtitles only — your ear will not train.

---

### Lesson 5 — Offline and weak internet (10 min)

- **Download** videos/audio on Wi-Fi (YouTube offline, podcast apps)
- Save **2–3 clips** for the week
- Repeat same clip **3 days** — deep learning beats endless new content
- Partner/sister: one downloads, shares file if safe and allowed

---

### Lesson 6 — Listening for exams and messages (Stage 2+)

- **Scan** for numbers, names, times
- **Signal words:** but (contrast), because (reason), so (result)
- Voice messages: listen twice before replying

---

## Examples

**What to write after listening (Level 3):**

> Topic: studying at home  
> Speaker: woman, American  
> Main idea: she studies 30 minutes daily  
> Words I heard: practice, improve, patient

**Dictation check:**

Audio: *“She wants to learn English for her future.”*  
Your write: *She wants to learn english for her future.*  
Fix: capitalize English; check *wants* ending.

---

## Practice tasks

1. One slow clip today — follow 5-step routine.
2. Dictate 5 sentences from [02-beginner-foundations.md](02-beginner-foundations.md) dialogues (read aloud yourself, or use resources).
3. Download one clip for offline repeat.
4. Drills: **[exercises/06-listening-drills.md](exercises/06-listening-drills.md)**

---

## Self-check questions

1. What is the 60% understanding rule?
2. Name the 5 steps of the daily routine.
3. Why should English subtitles beat Dari-only subtitles?
4. How many times should you reuse a good-level clip?
5. What do signal words *but* and *because* tell you?

---

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Only watching, not listening | Close screen sometimes — audio only |
| Always max speed | Start slow; speed up gradually |
| New clip every day | Repeat one clip 3 days |
| Giving up at 30% understanding | Choose easier level |

---

## Milestone goals

| When | Goal |
|------|------|
| Month 1 | 20 slow clips completed |
| Month 3 | Main idea without transcript on slow audio |
| Month 6 | 2-min natural speech with some replay |
| Month 12 | Podcast 10 min with notes |

- Same **time of day** trains your ear  
- Headphones help focus

# Speaking Skills

**Stage 1 → Stage 4** · Speak aloud every study day

---

## Learning goals

1. Speak **daily** even without a partner
2. Use **shadowing** (repeat with audio)
3. Build **self-talk** habits (describe your day)
4. Record voice **safely** on your phone
5. Grow from **survival** phrases to **2+ minute** talks

---

## Why this skill matters

English lives in your **mouth**, not only in your notebook. Speaking builds fluency and courage faster than silent study.

> *Your voice is valid even with mistakes. Communication beats perfection.*

---

## Level focus

**Stage 1:** phrases · **Stage 2:** 1–2 min · **Stage 3+:** opinions, stories

---

## Step-by-step lessons

### Lesson 1 — Speak from day 1 (10 min)

Rules:

- **Louder than whisper** — your mouth learns muscle memory
- **Slow is fine** — clear beats fast
- **Mistakes allowed** — fix one thing per day, not ten

**Today:** Say your introduction 5 times (from [02-beginner-foundations.md](02-beginner-foundations.md)).

---

### Lesson 2 — Shadowing (15 min)

1. Play one sentence (slow audio)
2. Pause
3. Repeat **exactly** — copy rhythm and stress
4. Record yourself
5. Compare once

**Starter sentences:**

- I am learning English step by step.
- Today I feel hopeful about my future.
- I study at home because I want a better life.

---

### Lesson 3 — Self-talk (15 min)

Narrate your real day in simple English:

> I wake up. I drink tea. I open my phone. I study English. I help my mother. I feel tired but happy.

**Levels:**

| Stage | Self-talk length |
|-------|------------------|
| 1 | 5 sentences |
| 2 | 10 sentences, past tense |
| 3 | opinions + reasons |
| 4 | abstract topics (hope, education, work) |

---

### Lesson 4 — Conversation patterns (20 min)

**Agree:** I agree. / That is true. / Me too.

**Disagree politely:** I see your point, but I think…

**Ask more:** Can you tell me more? / Why do you think that?

**Buy time:** Let me think… / That is a good question.

**Practice alone:** Play both sides — A and B voice.

---

### Lesson 5 — Recording safely on smartphone (10 min)

- Folder name: `English practice private`
- Delete old files monthly
- Do not post publicly without strong reason
- If sharing with sister/friend: trusted person only

**Weekly challenge:** 60-second voice note — topic: *my week*.

---

### Lesson 6 — Optional peer practice (safety first)

If you use apps or chat:

- No personal address or school location
- Public place not required for voice chat — **home is fine**
- Stop if someone is rude or pressures you
- Prefer female language partners when possible
- See safe app list in [15-resources.md](15-resources.md)

---

## Examples

**Stage 1 — 30 seconds:**

> Hello. My name is Maryam. I am 20 years old. I live in Afghanistan. I study English every day at home. I want to work online in the future. Thank you for listening.

**Stage 2 — 90 seconds (outline):**

- Past: Yesterday I studied grammar.
- Present: Today I feel motivated.
- Future: Tomorrow I will practice listening.
- Opinion: I think English opens doors for women.

# Reading Skills

**Stage 1 → Stage 4** · 10–15 min daily

---

## Learning goals

1. Use **phonics** (letters → sounds) at Stage 0–1
2. Move from **letters** to **graded texts**
3. Read **messages and short articles** with strategies
4. **Scan** for information (time, name, price)
5. **Infer** meaning from context
6. Build reading speed without skipping comprehension

---

## Why this skill matters

Reading feeds vocabulary and grammar at your speed. On a phone, you can read English **anywhere** — privacy, safety, independence.

> *Read a little every day. One paragraph beats zero pages.*

---

## Level focus

**Stage 1:** words/sentences · **Stage 2:** paragraphs · **Stage 3+:** articles, news

---

## Step-by-step lessons

### Lesson 0 — Phonics: letters to sounds (20 min)

**For Stage 0–1.** Reading English starts with **linking letters to sounds**.

**Consonants (common sounds):**

| Letter | Sound in | Example |
|--------|----------|---------|
| B | book | **b**ook |
| D | day | **d**ay |
| F | food | **f**ood |
| H | home | **h**ome |
| K | key | **k**ey |
| L | learn | **l**earn |
| M | mother | **m**other |
| N | name | **n**ame |
| S | study | **s**tudy |
| T | tea | **t**ea |

**Short vowels (practice slowly):**

| Letter | Sound | Word |
|--------|-------|------|
| A | /æ/ | c**a**t, h**a**t |
| E | /ɛ/ | b**e**d, r**e**d |
| I | /ɪ/ | s**i**t, b**i**g |
| O | /ɒ/ or /ɑ/ | h**o**t, g**o** (American varies) |
| U | /ʌ/ | c**u**p, s**u**n |

**Blends (two letters, one flow):**

- **sh** — she, wish  
- **ch** — child, teach  
- **th** — thank, this (see [05-pronunciation.md](05-pronunciation.md))  
- **st** — study, stop  
- **br** — bread, brother  

**Phone practice (5 min daily):**

1. Pick 5 words from [02-beginner-foundations.md](02-beginner-foundations.md).  
2. Say each **sound by sound**, then whole word.  
3. Type the word — connect **eyes → fingers → mouth**.

> **Dari note:** حروف لاتین صداهای متفاوت دارند — حرف *a* در *cat* و *name* یکسان نیست. Be patient; phonics takes 2–4 weeks.

**Simple decode steps:**

1. Look at first letter → say sound  
2. Move left to right  
3. Blend: *s* + *t* + *u* + *d* → *study*

When a word is long, split: *re-mem-ber* (later stage).

---

### Lesson 1 — Reading levels (10 min)

| Level | Text type |
|-------|-----------|
| A | Single words, labels |
| B | 1–3 sentences |
| C | Short dialogues |
| D | 100–200 word stories |
| E | News, blogs, chapters |

**Rule:** Know ~**80%** of words or guessable from context.

---

### Lesson 2 — Decode strategy (15 min)

For hard words:

1. Skip — read the sentence anyway  
2. Guess from context  
3. Look up only **key** words (do not stop every line)  
4. Write one English definition in your notebook

---

### Lesson 3 — Graded reading routine (20 min)

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Skim title and first sentence |
| 2 | Read full text once — no dictionary |
| 3 | Circle unknown words (mental note) |
| 4 | Read again — guess meanings |
| 5 | Write 2 sentences: main idea + my opinion |

---

### Lesson 4 — Scanning (15 min)

Find **fast**:

- phone number, email, date, price, name

**Practice text:**

> Class starts on **March 12** at **9:00 a.m.** Email **learn@example.com** for help. Fee: **free**.

Questions: When? What time? Email? Cost?

---

### Lesson 5 — Inference (15 min)

Text: *“Sara’s hands shook as she opened the test results. Then she smiled.”*

Infer: Sara was nervous; news was good.

**Practice:** Read short stories — ask *How did she feel? Why?*

---

### Lesson 6 — Phone reading habits (10 min)

- Increase font size for comfort  
- Night mode for eyes  
- Bookmark one article — finish tomorrow (not 10 half-read)  
- Download PDF/HTML for offline ([15-resources.md](15-resources.md))

---

## Sample graded text (Stage 1–2)

**My Study Corner**

I study English in a small room at home. I use my phone and a notebook. I learn thirty minutes every day. Some days I am tired, but I continue. My sister sometimes listens and smiles. I want to read books in English one day. I know patience is important. Every word I learn is a step forward.

**Comprehension (try before keys in exercises):**

1. Where does she study?
2. How long each day?
3. Who listens sometimes?
4. What is her dream?

---

## Practice tasks

1. Read sample text twice; answer questions.
2. Scan a real message (email/chat) for one fact.
3. Write 3 sentences summarizing your day (reading + writing link).
4. Drills: **[exercises/08-reading-drills.md](exercises/08-reading-drills.md)**

---

## Self-check questions

1. What is the 80% word rule?
2. What are the 5 steps of graded reading?
3. What is scanning vs deep reading?
4. What is inference?
5. Why not look up every unknown word?

---

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Text too hard | Drop one level |
| Translating every word in head | Summarize in simple English |
| No re-read | Second read builds speed |
| Only social media slang | Add one graded paragraph/week |

# Writing Skills

**Stage 1 → Stage 4** · 10–15 min daily

---

## Learning goals

1. Write **correct simple sentences** (SVO order)
2. Compose **chat-length** and **email-length** messages
3. Build **paragraphs** with topic sentence + details
4. Use **punctuation** and capitalization
5. Edit your own work with a **simple checklist**

---

## Why this skill matters

Writing makes thinking clear. It helps you apply for courses, send professional messages, and express yourself when speaking is not possible.

> *Write messy first. Edit second. Courage first, perfection later.*

---

## Level focus

**Stage 1:** sentences · **Stage 2:** paragraphs · **Stage 3+:** formal writing

---

## Step-by-step lessons

### Lesson 1 — Sentence skeleton (15 min)

**Subject + Verb + Object** (American order)

| Part | Example |
|------|---------|
| Subject | I |
| Verb | study |
| Object | English |

- I study English. ✓  
- Study I English. ✗

> **Dari note:** Dari allows flexible order. English is stricter — subject usually **first**.

---

### Lesson 2 — Capitalization and punctuation (15 min)

**Capitalize:**

- First word of sentence
- I
- Names, places: Fatima, Afghanistan, Monday
- Language: English

**Punctuation:**

| Mark | Use |
|------|-----|
| . | end of statement |
| ? | question |
| ! | strong feeling (use rarely) |
| , | short pause in list or clause |

**Example:**

> Hello, my name is Laila. I live in Afghanistan. Do you study English?

---

### Lesson 3 — Chat writing (10 min)

Short, clear, polite:

> Good morning! I am fine today. I studied English for 30 minutes. How are you?  

Avoid: all caps (looks angry), too many !!! 

---

### Lesson 4 — Daily journal (15 min)

**Template (5 sentences):**

1. Today I…  
2. I felt…  
3. I learned…  
4. Tomorrow I will…  
5. I am grateful for…

Optional: journal helps emotions — keep private.

---

### Lesson 5 — Paragraph structure (20 min)

**One paragraph = 5–8 sentences**

1. **Topic sentence** — main idea  
2. **Detail** — example or reason  
3. **Detail** — second example  
4. **Closing** — summary or feeling

**Example topic:** Why I study English

> I study English because it gives me independence. I can take online courses and read global news. I practice thirty minutes every day at home on my phone. Some days are hard, but I continue. English is a door to my future, and I choose to open it.

---

### Lesson 6 — Linking words (15 min)

| Type | Words |
|------|-------|
| Add | and, also, moreover |
| Contrast | but, however, although |
| Reason | because, since |
| Order | first, then, finally |
| Example | for example, such as |

---

### Lesson 7 — Self-editing checklist (10 min)

Before you finish:

- [ ] Every sentence starts with capital
- [ ] Subject + verb match (she **goes**, not she go)
- [ ] Period at end
- [ ] *I* is capital
- [ ] Read aloud — does it sound right?

---

## Examples

**Stage 1 — 5 sentences about today:**

> I woke up early. I helped my family. I studied English on my phone. I learned ten new words. I feel proud tonight.

**Stage 2 — email (informal):**

> Subject: Question about the online course  
>  
> Dear Teacher,  
>  
> My name is Hawa. I am interested in your free English course. Can you tell me how to register? Thank you for your time.  
>  
> Best regards,  
> Hawa

---

## Practice tasks

1. Write today’s journal (5 sentences).
2. Write one paragraph: *My study routine*.
3. Edit yesterday’s writing with the checklist.
4. Drills: **[exercises/09-writing-drills.md](exercises/09-writing-drills.md)**

---

## Self-check questions

1. What is SVO order?
2. When do you capitalize letters?
3. What is a topic sentence?
4. Name three linking words for contrast and reason.
5. What are five items on the editing checklist?

---

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| i instead of I | Always capital I |
| Run-on sentences (no period) | Split into two sentences |
| Missing articles | Add a/the where needed ([03-core-grammar.md](03-core-grammar.md)) |
| Dari word order | Read aloud — if odd, reorder |

---

| When | Goal |
|------|------|
| Week 2 | 5 correct sentences daily |
| Month 1 | One paragraph (80 words) |
| Month 3 | Email format correct |
| Month 6 | Essay 150+ words with linking words |

# Intermediate Growth (B1 → B2)

**Stage 2** · Months 4–8

---

## Learning goals

1. Use **past tense** for stories and experiences
2. Use **future** forms (will, going to, present continuous for plans)
3. Express **opinions** and **reasons**
4. Connect ideas with **linking words**
5. Speak and write **2–5 minutes** on familiar topics

---

## Why this skill matters

Beginner English describes *now*. Intermediate English describes *your life story* and *your plans* — past hardships, future dreams, present choices.

> *You are no longer “studying English.” You are using English to live and think.*

---

## Level focus

**Stage 2** (B1 → B2 bridge).

**Prerequisite:** Solid Stage 1 — present tense, 300+ words, basic listening.

---

## Step-by-step lessons

### Lesson 1 — Past simple: regular verbs (20 min)

**Form:** verb + **-ed** (walk → walked)

| Affirmative | Negative | Question |
|-------------|----------|----------|
| I walked | I did not walk | Did you walk? |
| She studied | She did not study | Did she study? |

**Spelling rules:**

- stop → stopped (double consonant)
- try → tried (y → ied)
- play → played

**Examples:**

> Yesterday I **studied** English for forty minutes.  
> Last year I **started** learning at home.  
> She **helped** her sister with homework.

---

### Lesson 2 — Past simple: irregular verbs (25 min)

Learn in **groups of 5 per week**:

| Base | Past |
|------|------|
| go | went |
| see | saw |
| make | made |
| take | took |
| come | came |
| get | got |
| know | knew |
| think | thought |
| say | said |
| feel | felt |
| have | had |
| be | was / were |

**Story practice:**

> Last week I **went** online. I **saw** a free course. I **took** notes. I **felt** nervous but happy.

---

### Lesson 3 — Past continuous (15 min)

**Was/were + verb-ing** — action in progress in the past

> I **was studying** when my mother called.  
> They **were watching** a lesson at 8 p.m.

**Contrast:**

> I **studied** (finished action) vs I **was studying** (in progress)

---

### Lesson 4 — Future forms (20 min)

| Form | Use | Example |
|------|-----|---------|
| **will** | decision, promise, prediction | I **will** practice tomorrow. |
| **going to** | plan already decided | I am **going to** finish Module 10. |
| **present continuous** | fixed arrangement | I am **meeting** my sister tonight to study. |

---

### Lesson 5 — Modals for ability and advice (20 min)

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| can / could | ability | I **can** read slow English. |
| should | advice | You **should** review old words. |
| must / have to | necessity | I **must** rest when I am sick. |
| may / might | possibility | It **might** rain; I will study inside. |

---

### Lesson 6 — Opinions (15 min)

| Phrase | Example |
|--------|---------|
| I think… | I think daily practice works. |
| In my opinion… | In my opinion, women should have education. |
| I believe… | I believe hope is important. |
| I agree / disagree | I disagree with stopping after one hard day. |

**Structure:** Opinion + reason + example

> **I think** English opens opportunities. **Because** many courses are online. **For example**, I found free grammar lessons.

---

### Lesson 7 — Connectors and storytelling (20 min)

**Timeline words:** first, then, after that, later, finally

**Contrast:** however, although, but

**Cause:** because, since, so

**Mini story (90 sec spoken):**

> **First**, I was afraid to speak. **Then** I recorded my voice every week. **After that**, I understood more listening clips. **Finally**, I spoke for two minutes without stopping. **Although** I still make mistakes, I continue.

---

### Lesson 8 — Fluency bridges (15 min)

**Fillers (natural, not too many):**

- Well, … / Let me see, … / I mean, …

**Rephrase when stuck:**

> Sorry, let me say that another way. …  
> What I want to say is, …

**Practice:** 2-minute talk on *a challenge I overcame*.

---

## Examples

**Past + opinion paragraph:**

> Last month I had a difficult week. I did not study for three days because I was tired. However, I started again on Monday. I think consistency matters more than perfection. I am going to study thirty minutes daily this month. I believe small steps change lives.

---

## Practice tasks

1. Write 10 past tense sentences about last week.
2. Write 5 future sentences (will + going to).
3. Record 2-minute story with timeline connectors.
4. Drills: **[exercises/10-intermediate-drills.md](exercises/10-intermediate-drills.md)**

---

## Self-check questions

1. How do you form past tense for regular verbs?
2. Past of *go*, *see*, *have*?
3. When use *will* vs *going to*?
4. Write one opinion + because + example.
5. Name four timeline connectors.

---

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| I go yesterday | I **went** yesterday |
| Did you went? | Did you **go**? |
| I will going | I **am going to** go / I **will** go |
| No connector in long speech | Add first, then, finally |

| Month 8 | B1 checkpoint quiz pass ([14-assessments.md](14-assessments.md)) |

# Advanced Skills (B2 → C2)

**Stage 3 → Stage 4** · Months 9–24+

---

## Learning goals

1. Build **complex sentences** (clauses, conditionals)
2. Control **register** (formal vs informal)
3. Write and speak **professionally** (email, presentations)
4. Use **hedging** and **nuance** (maybe, tend to, it appears)
5. Argue a position **clearly and politely**

---

## Why this skill matters

Advanced English is not “more grammar rules.” It is **precision**, **respect**, and **power** — in school applications, remote work, advocacy, and global conversation.

> *You have something to say. Advanced English helps the world hear it clearly.*

---

## Level focus

**Stage 3–4** (B2 → C1 → C2).

---

## Step-by-step lessons

### Lesson 1 — Relative clauses (20 min)

**Who** — people · **which** — things · **that** — both (informal)

> The woman **who** teaches online inspires me.  
> The course **which** I took was free.  
> The book **that** I read was difficult.

**Reduced:** The woman teaching online… (advanced style)

---

### Lesson 2 — Conditionals (25 min)

| Type | Structure | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Zero | If + present, present | If you heat ice, it melts. |
| First | If + present, will | If I practice, I **will** improve. |
| Second | If + past, would | If I **had** time, I **would** study more. |
| Third | If + past perfect, would have | If I **had started** earlier, I **would have learned** more. |

**Your life:**

> If I study daily, I will reach B2.  
> If I had fluent English now, I would apply for more scholarships.

---

### Lesson 3 — Passive voice (15 min)

**Be + past participle** — focus on action/object

> English **is taught** online.  
> The message **was sent** yesterday.  
> Mistakes **are made** — that is normal.

---

### Lesson 4 — Register: formal vs informal (20 min)

| Informal | Formal |
|----------|--------|
| Hi | Dear / Hello |
| Thanks a lot | Thank you for your assistance |
| I want | I would like |
| ASAP | as soon as possible |
| kids | children |

**Rule:** Job, university, unknown adults → **formal**. Friends, sisters → **informal**.

---

### Lesson 5 — Hedging and nuance (15 min)

Avoid sounding too absolute in academic/professional speech:

| Hedge | Example |
|-------|---------|
| may, might | This **may** help learners. |
| tend to | Women **tend to** face more barriers. |
| it seems / appears | It **appears** that practice works. |
| in many cases | In many cases, small groups help. |

---

### Lesson 6 — Argument structure (25 min)

1. **Claim** — main point  
2. **Evidence** — fact or example  
3. **Counter** — other view (optional)  
4. **Reply** — why your claim still holds  
5. **Conclusion**

**Topic:** Education should be available to all women.

> **Claim:** Every woman deserves access to education. **Evidence:** Studies show education improves health and income. **Counter:** Some say resources are limited. **Reply:** Long-term investment in women strengthens communities. **Conclusion:** Restrictions harm society; education is a right.

---

### Lesson 7 — Professional email (20 min)

**Structure:**

- Subject line (clear)
- Greeting (Dear Ms. … / Dear Team)
- Purpose (first paragraph)
- Details (bullet points if needed)
- Polite close (Thank you for your consideration)
- Signature (name, optional contact)

---

### Lesson 8 — Presentations (20 min)

**Outline (5–7 min talk):**

1. Hook — question or short story  
2. Problem  
3. Your main points (3 bullets)  
4. Solution or hope  
5. Call to action — what listener can do  
6. Thank you

**Practice:** Record on phone; watch once; note filler words to reduce.

---

## Examples

**Formal sentence:**

> I am writing to inquire about the registration deadline for the online certificate program.

**Nuanced opinion:**

> Although progress takes time, it seems that consistent self-study can lead to strong results, especially when learners face limited classroom access.

# Study Strategies for Long-Term Success

**All stages** · Read early; revisit after breaks

---

## Learning goals

1. Build **consistency** without burnout
2. **Restart** after gaps without shame
3. Create a **safe** study environment
4. Use **motivation** tools that work in hard times
5. Study with a **sister circle** safely (optional)

---

## Why this skill matters

Skills fade without habits. Strategies keep you learning when life interrupts school, internet, or confidence.

> *Discipline is remembering what you want. Courage is studying anyway.*

---

## Level focus

**All stages** — especially after breaks.

---

## Step-by-step lessons

### Lesson 1 — The 3 pillars (15 min)

| Pillar | Action |
|--------|--------|
| **Time** | Same window most days (even 15 min) |
| **Method** | Rotate skills (V/G/L/S/R/W) |
| **Mindset** | Kind self-talk |

Missing one pillar? Fix the weakest — usually mindset after a hard week.

---

### Lesson 2 — Motivation that lasts (20 min)

**Weak motivation:** “I must be fluent in 3 months.”  
**Strong motivation:** “Today I will learn 10 words and use them.”

**Write your why** (keep private):

- For my education
- For remote work
- To read books
- To help my family
- To prove I can

**Read your why** every Sunday.

**Visual wins:** checklist in [16-tracker.md](16-tracker.md) — tick marks release dopamine.

---

### Lesson 3 — Recovery after interruption (15 min)

**If you stopped 1–7 days:**

1. Do **not** study double time to “catch up”
2. Open tracker — read last win
3. Study **15 minutes** today only
4. Return to normal plan tomorrow

**If you stopped 1+ months:**

1. Re-take placement quiz ([14-assessments.md](14-assessments.md))
2. Review **one** module below your level (confidence)
3. Start **12-week plan** again at Week 1 rhythm — content may be faster second time

> **Shame is not a study method.** Restart is.

---

### Lesson 4 — Study space safety (15 min)

- Chair or cushion, good light if possible
- Phone on silent except study app
- Water nearby
- Tell one trusted person “I study now” if that protects your time
- If interrupted often, use **headphones** + short sessions

**Digital:** separate folder for English; avoid mixing with stressful chats mid-session.

---

### Lesson 5 — Sister study circle (optional, 20 min)

**Safe circle rules:**

- 2–4 trusted women only
- Meet by voice/chat at home — no unsafe travel required
- Agenda: 30 min English only
- Rotate: one teaches vocabulary, one asks questions
- No criticism of accent — one correction per person per session
- Share **resources**, not personal danger stories

**Weekly format:**

| Min | Activity |
|-----|----------|
| 5 | Warm-up: each says one sentence |
| 10 | Quiz each other on words |
| 10 | Read one paragraph aloud |
| 5 | Plan homework for alone days |

---

### Lesson 6 — Anti-burnout rules (10 min)

- **One day off** per week is OK
- **Max 45 min** if tired — quality over quantity
- Switch skill if bored (grammar → listening)
- Sleep and food matter for memory
- Celebrate **months studied**, not only level jumps

---

### Lesson 7 — Common learning myths (10 min)

| Myth | Truth |
|------|-------|
| “Adults cannot learn” | Adults learn well with strategy |
| “Accent must be perfect” | Clarity beats accent |
| “Grammar first, speak later” | Speak small from day 1 |
| “One app is enough” | Rotate skills and sources |
| “Missing a day = failure” | Habit returns with one session |

---

## Examples

**Monday self-talk after a hard week:**

> I stopped for five days. That is human. Today I review ten words and listen to one clip. I am still a learner. I am still moving forward.

**Sister circle message:**

> Sisters, tomorrow 4 p.m. we practice English 30 minutes. Bring three new words. We will read paragraph 2 from Reading module together.

## Practice tasks

1. Write your *why* in 2–3 sentences.
2. Plan your safe study corner (draw or list).
3. If you had a break, do 15-minute restart today.
4. Optional: invite one trusted friend to 30-min English chat.

5. What is a good goal for a tired day?

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| All grammar, no joy | Add favorite topic weekly |
| Studying 3 hours once, then zero | Small daily beats rare marathons |
| Comparing to native children online | Compare to your tracker |
| Isolating completely | One safe partner helps accountability |

# Assessments and Progress Checkpoints

**All stages** · Try first · Keys in [answer-keys/](answer-keys/)

---

## Learning goals

1. Find your **starting level** (placement)
2. Check progress at **4 checkpoints**
3. Score **speaking** and **writing** with rubrics
4. Know when to **move up** a stage
5. Use results to **adjust** your study plan

---

## Why assessment matters

Tests are not punishment. They are a **mirror** — they show what to study next.

> *A low score today is data, not destiny.*

---

## Placement quiz (25 questions)

**Instructions:** 45 minutes max. No dictionary. No answer keys until finished.

Check answers: **[answer-keys/14-placement-quiz-KEYS.md](answer-keys/14-placement-quiz-KEYS.md)**

### Part A — Foundations (1–8)

1. Write the English alphabet in order (or recite and write).
2. What is 14 in English words?
3. Complete: She ___ a student. (am / is / are)
4. Complete: They ___ English every day. (study / studies)
5. Negative: He likes tea. → He ___ ___ tea.
6. Question: You live in Kabul. → ___ you live in Kabul?
7. Choose: There ___ two books. (is / are)
8. Translate idea to English (write one sentence): *I thank you.*

### Part B — Grammar & vocab (9–16)

9. Past of *go*: ___
10. Complete: I ___ studying when she called. (was / were / am)
11. Future: I ___ visit tomorrow. (will / am will / wills)
12. Modal: You ___ rest if you are sick. (should / must both OK — choose one)
13. Opposite of *hot*: ___
14. Write one sentence with *because*.
15. Article: I need ___ pen. (a / an / the)
16. Write 5 words on the topic *study*.

### Part C — Intermediate (17–22)

17. Past participle used with *have*: I have ___ (finish) → ___
18. Second conditional: If I ___ (have) more time, I ___ (study) more.
19. Relative clause: The woman ___ teaches online is kind. (who / which)
20. Formal: Choose better: *I want help* OR *I would like assistance*
21. Write 2 sentences: past + future about your English learning.
22. Connector: Complete with one word: I was tired, ___ I continued.

### Part D — Advanced (23–25)

23. Passive: English ___ (teach) in many countries.
24. Hedge: Rewrite softer: *English is easy.* → ___
25. Write 3–4 sentences: opinion on why education matters for women.

### Scoring guide

| Score | Level | Start module |
|-------|-------|--------------|
| 0–8 | Stage 0–1 | [02-beginner-foundations.md](02-beginner-foundations.md) |
| 9–16 | Stage 1–2 | [03-core-grammar.md](03-core-grammar.md) + [10-intermediate-growth.md](10-intermediate-growth.md) when ready |
| 17–22 | Stage 2–3 | [10-intermediate-growth.md](10-intermediate-growth.md) |
| 23–25 | Stage 3–4 | [11-advanced-skills.md](11-advanced-skills.md) |

## Grammar

| English | Dari hint | Note |
|---------|-----------|------|
| **a / an** | یک (yak) — general one | English requires *a book*, not just *book* |
| **the** | آن / همان (specific) | When both know which thing |
| **am / is / are** | هست (hast) — but English **never drops** | Wrong: *I fine* → *I am fine* |
| **have / has** | داشتن (dâštan) | *I have time* = وقت دارم |
| **do / does** (questions) | — | Helper verb; Dari uses different question form |
| **-s** (he goes) | — | Third person always adds **-s** in present |
| **there is / are** | وجود دارد | *There is a book* = کتاب هست |

## Pronunciation

| Sound | Dari note | Practice word |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| **th** (thank) | Between teeth; not ت or س | thank, three |
| **th** (this) | Voiced; vibration | this, mother |
| **v** | شبیه و in some words | very — teeth on lip |
| **p vs f** | پ vs ف | pat vs fat |
| **short i vs long ee** | ش vs شی | ship vs sheep |

|-----------------|------------|

| Adjective before noun | **red** book (not book red) |

**Questions:** 1. How long did she study? 2. Name three words. 3. What will she do tomorrow? 4. When does she want to study together? 5. Who is the message for?

---

## Reading 3 — Level C (Pre-intermediate)

**Studying When Life Is Hard**

Last year, Fatima stopped school for several months. She felt sad, but she did not quit English. She studied at home every day when she had time. Sometimes she studied only fifteen minutes. She used free websites and downloaded audio files.

Now Fatima can write a paragraph about her day. She believes small steps are stronger than one long day per month. Her mother is proud. Fatima wants to take an online course next year.

**Questions:** 1. Did she quit English? 2. How long on bad days? 3. What can she do now? 4. Who is proud? 5. What is her plan for next year?

---

## Reading 4 — Level D (Intermediate)

**Remote Work and Language**

Many young women learn English because they want remote work. Remote jobs need clear writing and calm video calls. You do not need perfect grammar on day one, but you need consistent practice.

First, build a daily habit. Second, record your voice and listen. Third, write short emails to yourself. Fourth, read news in easy English. Finally, apply for small tasks when you feel ready.

However, protect your privacy online. Do not share your address. Use trusted platforms. If a job offer looks too good, it may be a scam.

**Questions:** 1. Why learn English for work? 2. List two skills for remote jobs. 3. What are four steps in the text? 4. What privacy warning is given? 5. What is the main idea?

---

## Reading 5 — Level E (Upper intermediate)

**Education and Hope**

Education is not only a building with desks. It is also a lamp in a dark room. When classrooms close, learning can continue on a phone, in a notebook, or in a sister circle at home.

Researchers often report that literacy improves health and income over time. Although resources are limited, women who read and write gain more control over information. They can verify news, understand medicine labels, and support their children.

Therefore, self-study is not a small choice. It is an act of hope. Every word you learn today may help you tomorrow — and the next year, and the next decade.

**Questions:** 1. Metaphor: education is a ___? 2. Name two benefits of literacy from the text. 3. What can women verify? 4. What is self-study compared to? 5. Write one sentence agreeing or disagreeing.

---

## Reading 6 — Level B (Health)

**A Simple Day**

Sara wakes up at six. She drinks warm water and tea. She does not feel well, so she rests for one hour. Later she studies English for twenty minutes. She eats rice and vegetables for lunch. In the evening, she walks in the house and stretches her arms. She goes to bed early. She says, "Rest is also study for the body."

**Questions:** 1. What time does she wake up? 2. Why does she rest one hour? 3. How long does she study? 4. What does she eat? 5. What is her quote about rest?

---

## Reading 7 — Level C (Technology)

**My Phone Is My Classroom**

When classrooms are far or closed, my phone becomes my classroom. I download lessons on good Wi-Fi days. I save vocabulary in my notes app. I record my voice and listen again. Sometimes the battery is low, so I plan charging time like I plan study time. I do not open unsafe links. I use passwords my family does not know. English on my phone is freedom with responsibility.

**Questions:** 1. What is her classroom? 2. When does she download? 3. Name two study actions on phone. 4. Two safety habits? 5. Main idea in one sentence?

---

## Reading 8 — Level D (Opinion)

**Should Women Study Alone at Home?**

Some people say women should not study alone. I disagree. Home study can be safe and strong when the family supports education. A woman can learn grammar, listen to podcasts, and write emails without traveling. However, she should protect her privacy online and choose trusted apps.

In my opinion, the question is not "alone or not alone." The question is: does she have time, safety, and hope? If yes, home study can change her future.

**Questions:** 1. What do some people say? 2. Does the writer agree? 3. Name two skills learned at home. 4. One online safety rule? 5. What is the writer's real question?

---

## Reading 9 — Level D (Narrative past)

**The Day I Spoke English**

Last Friday I was nervous. I opened my notebook and read six sentences aloud. My sister listened from the kitchen. At first I forgot a word, but I continued. I said, "I study because I want a better future." My sister smiled and said, "Good job." That small moment was my victory. I recorded it on my phone. I will listen again when I feel afraid.

**Questions:** 1. When? 2. Who listened? 3. What problem happened? 4. What sentence did she say? 5. Why will she listen to the recording?

---

## Reading 10 — Level E (Argument)

**Language as a Tool, Not a Trophy**

Fluent accents attract attention on social media, but education is not a performance. For many Afghan women, English is a **tool**: to read health information, to apply for scholarships, to work online, and to teach younger sisters.

Although perfect pronunciation is admirable, **communication** matters more. Learners should measure progress by tasks they can do today — write an email, understand a headline, speak for two minutes — not by comparison with native speakers.

Therefore, policy and families should support daily practice, safe internet use, and patience. Language opens doors only when the learner keeps walking.

**Questions:** 1. English is a tool for what four things? 2. What matters more than perfect accent? 3. Give two progress measures. 4. What should families support? 5. Write 2-sentence summary.

# Building Fluency

Use with [07-speaking.md](../07-speaking.md), [10-intermediate-growth.md](../10-intermediate-growth.md), [13-practice-plans.md](../13-practice-plans.md).

---

## What fluency means here

Fluency is **smooth, understandable speech** — not zero mistakes. You can be fluent at B1 and still learn until C2.

> *Fluency grows when your mouth practices English as often as your eyes.*

---

## The 4 fluency engines

| Engine | Daily action | Minutes |
|--------|----------------|---------|
| Input | Listen to clear English | 10 |
| Output | Speak aloud (self-talk) | 10 |
| Feedback | Record + listen once | 5 |
| Review | Repeat yesterday’s phrases | 5 |

---

## Week 1–4 fluency challenge (beginner)

| Week | Challenge |
|------|-----------|
| 1 | Say 5 sentences about morning — no writing |
| 2 | Shadow 10 lines from slow audio |
| 3 | 60-sec voice note daily |
| 4 | Describe a photo in 8 sentences |

---

## Week 5–12 (intermediate)

| Week | Challenge |
|------|-----------|
| 5–6 | Tell past story 90 sec |
| 7–8 | Opinion + because + example 2 min |
| 9–10 | Summarize a short article aloud |
| 11–12 | Role-play both sides of a dialogue |

---

## Connector pack (paste in Notes)

First, … / Then, … / After that, … / However, … / Because, … / For example, … / In my opinion, … / Finally, …

**Drill:** Use 4 connectors in one 2-minute talk.

---

## When you freeze

1. Say: *Let me think.*  
2. Say idea in **simple** words.  
3. Use gesture if with sister (optional).  
4. Do **not** switch to Dari mid-sentence if goal is English mode.

# Building Confidence in English

Use with [07-speaking.md](../07-speaking.md), [12-study-strategies.md](../12-study-strategies.md).

---

## Confidence is a skill

Confidence is not a gift. It is **practice + kind self-talk + proof of progress**.

> *You are allowed to sound like a learner. Learners become fluent.*

---

## 7 confidence rules

1. **Speak before you feel ready** — feeling follows action.  
2. **One mistake per correction** — not ten at once.  
3. **Record in private** — proof beats memory.  
4. **Compare to last month** — not to native speakers online.  
5. **Celebrate speaking days** — mark ✓ in tracker.  
6. **Rest without guilt** — return tomorrow.  
7. **Your voice has value** — ideas matter more than accent.

---

## Shame recovery script (read aloud)

> I stopped for a while. That is human. Today I study fifteen minutes. I am still a learner. I am still moving forward. I do not need permission to try again.

Write your own version in English. Read every Monday.

---

## Weekly confidence tasks

| Day | Task |
|-----|------|
| Mon | Write 1 thing you did well in English |
| Wed | Speak 5 sentences without reading |
| Fri | Record 60 sec — delete if you want after listening |
| Sun | Read tracker wins from the week |

---

## Safe speaking environments

| Safe | Less safe |
|------|-----------|
| Alone in room | Public criticism |
| Trusted sister | Strangers judging accent |
| Voice memo private folder | Posting recordings publicly without reason |

---

## When someone mocks your English

You may say: *I am learning.* Then leave the conversation mentally. Do not carry their words into study time.
