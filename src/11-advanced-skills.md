# Advanced Skills (B2 → C2)

**Stage 3 → Stage 4** · Months 9–24+

---

## Learning goals

1. Build **complex sentences** (clauses, conditionals)
2. Control **register** (formal vs informal)
3. Write and speak **professionally** (email, presentations)
4. Use **hedging** and **nuance** (maybe, tend to, it appears)
5. Argue a position **clearly and politely**

---

## Why this skill matters

Advanced English is not “more grammar rules.” It is **precision**, **respect**, and **power** — in school applications, remote work, advocacy, and global conversation.

> *You have something to say. Advanced English helps the world hear it clearly.*

---

## Level focus

**Stage 3–4** (B2 → C1 → C2).

---

## Step-by-step lessons

### Lesson 1 — Relative clauses (20 min)

**Who** — people · **which** — things · **that** — both (informal)

> The woman **who** teaches online inspires me.  
> The course **which** I took was free.  
> The book **that** I read was difficult.

**Reduced:** The woman teaching online… (advanced style)

---

### Lesson 2 — Conditionals (25 min)

| Type | Structure | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Zero | If + present, present | If you heat ice, it melts. |
| First | If + present, will | If I practice, I **will** improve. |
| Second | If + past, would | If I **had** time, I **would** study more. |
| Third | If + past perfect, would have | If I **had started** earlier, I **would have learned** more. |

**Your life:**

> If I study daily, I will reach B2.  
> If I had fluent English now, I would apply for more scholarships.

---

### Lesson 3 — Passive voice (15 min)

**Be + past participle** — focus on action/object

> English **is taught** online.  
> The message **was sent** yesterday.  
> Mistakes **are made** — that is normal.

---

### Lesson 4 — Register: formal vs informal (20 min)

| Informal | Formal |
|----------|--------|
| Hi | Dear / Hello |
| Thanks a lot | Thank you for your assistance |
| I want | I would like |
| ASAP | as soon as possible |
| kids | children |

**Rule:** Job, university, unknown adults → **formal**. Friends, sisters → **informal**.

---

### Lesson 5 — Hedging and nuance (15 min)

Avoid sounding too absolute in academic/professional speech:

| Hedge | Example |
|-------|---------|
| may, might | This **may** help learners. |
| tend to | Women **tend to** face more barriers. |
| it seems / appears | It **appears** that practice works. |
| in many cases | In many cases, small groups help. |

---

### Lesson 6 — Argument structure (25 min)

1. **Claim** — main point  
2. **Evidence** — fact or example  
3. **Counter** — other view (optional)  
4. **Reply** — why your claim still holds  
5. **Conclusion**

**Topic:** Education should be available to all women.

> **Claim:** Every woman deserves access to education. **Evidence:** Studies show education improves health and income. **Counter:** Some say resources are limited. **Reply:** Long-term investment in women strengthens communities. **Conclusion:** Restrictions harm society; education is a right.

---

### Lesson 7 — Professional email (20 min)

**Structure:**

- Subject line (clear)
- Greeting (Dear Ms. … / Dear Team)
- Purpose (first paragraph)
- Details (bullet points if needed)
- Polite close (Thank you for your consideration)
- Signature (name, optional contact)

---

### Lesson 8 — Presentations (20 min)

**Outline (5–7 min talk):**

1. Hook — question or short story  
2. Problem  
3. Your main points (3 bullets)  
4. Solution or hope  
5. Call to action — what listener can do  
6. Thank you

**Practice:** Record on phone; watch once; note filler words to reduce.

---

## Examples

**Formal sentence:**

> I am writing to inquire about the registration deadline for the online certificate program.

**Nuanced opinion:**

> Although progress takes time, it seems that consistent self-study can lead to strong results, especially when learners face limited classroom access.

---

## Practice tasks

1. Write 5 sentences with *who/which/that*.
2. Write 3 first conditional + 2 second conditional sentences about your goals.
3. Draft one professional email (real or practice).
4. Record 3-minute presentation outline on *why I learn English*.

---

## Self-check questions

1. When use *who* vs *which*?
2. Give an example of first vs second conditional.
3. What is hedging and why use it?
4. Name five parts of an argument.
5. What is different in formal email greeting?

---

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Too informal in job email | Use Dear, would like, thank you |
| Too formal with close friend | Relax tone |
| Every sentence passive | Mix active + passive |
| No evidence in arguments | Add one fact or personal example |
