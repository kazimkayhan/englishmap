# Overview: Your English Journey

---

## Welcome

You are holding a complete path from **no English** to **strong, confident English**. This roadmap was written for **young women in Afghanistan** who want to learn **American English** through **self-study** — often at home, often on a phone, often without a teacher.

If you can read this, you are already learning.

> **Remember:** A broken schedule is not a broken dream. You can restart any day.

---

## Who this roadmap serves

- **Age:** About 18–25 (young adult topics and respect)
- **First language:** Dari
- **Time:** About 30–45 minutes per day
- **Device:** Smartphone
- **English type:** American English
- **Mode:** Self-study (no teacher required)

---

## What “mastery” means here

Mastery does not mean perfection. It means you can:

- Understand everyday American speech at normal speed
- Express your ideas clearly in speech and writing
- Learn new English on your own (videos, articles, courses)
- Use English for **your goals** — study, work online, help family, connect with the world

That is a realistic, powerful kind of mastery.

---

## Five stages (0 → 4)

| Stage | Name | Time (30–45 min/day) | You can… |
|-------|------|----------------------|----------|
| **0** | Pre-beginner | Weeks 1–2 | Read the alphabet, say hello, use 50 survival words |
| **1** | Beginner (A1–A2) | Months 1–3 | Talk about daily life in present tense, write short messages |
| **2** | Intermediate (B1) | Months 4–8 | Tell stories in past tense, share opinions, write paragraphs |
| **3** | Upper intermediate (B2–C1) | Months 9–14 | Discuss abstract topics, read longer texts, write formal emails |
| **4** | Advanced (C1–C2) | Months 15–24+ | Use nuance, persuade, present, write professionally |

**Honest truth:** Life in difficult circumstances may slow you down. The roadmap still works if you study **3 days a week** — just extend the calendar.

## Safety and privacy

### Study space

- Choose a place where you feel **safe and focused** (home corner, quiet room).
- Use **headphones** when listening so others are not disturbed.
- If someone questions your study, you may say simply: *“I am learning a skill for my future.”* You do not owe a long explanation.

### Digital safety

- Use **strong passwords** on learning accounts.
- Be careful with **voice recordings** — store in a private folder; delete old files.
- Do not share personal location or contact details in language exchange apps.
- Prefer **well-known free apps** listed in **[15-resources.md](15-resources.md)**.

### Emotional safety

- Compare yourself to **your past self**, not to influencers or fluent speakers online.
- Shame slows learning. **Curiosity speeds it up.**
- Rest days are allowed. A rest day is not quitting.

## Daily time: how to use 30–45 minutes

### Option A — One block (40 minutes)

| Minutes | Activity |
|---------|----------|
| 5 | Review yesterday’s words |
| 15 | Main lesson (module or video) |
| 10 | Exercises |
| 10 | Speaking or writing out loud |

### Option B — Three blocks (15 + 15 + 15)

| Block | Activity |
|-------|----------|
| Morning | Vocabulary + pronunciation |
| Afternoon | Grammar + writing |
| Evening | Listening + repeat aloud |

### Busy-week minimum (15 minutes)

1. Review 10 words  
2. Listen to one short clip  
3. Say five sentences out loud  

**Fifteen minutes keeps your habit alive.**

## Dari speaker advantage

You already speak a rich language. English will feel strange at first — **that is normal**.

Common differences (you will learn fixes in modules):

| Dari | English |
|------|---------|
| No articles (*a*, *the*) | Articles required |
| Verb often at end | Subject–verb–object order |
| Different vowel sounds | New vowel pairs (ship/sheep) |
| Persian script vs Latin alphabet | New writing system at start |

Your brain can learn both. Millions of Dari speakers have learned English — you are on the same path.
