# Memory Techniques for Vocabulary

**Learning goal:** Remember new words faster and longer

---

## Why memory techniques matter

Learning 1000+ words requires **smart repetition**, not just hard work. Memory techniques help you:
- Remember words after fewer repetitions
- Keep words in long-term memory
- Connect new words to what you already know
- Make learning more enjoyable

> **Key principle:** Your brain remembers stories, patterns, and emotions better than random lists.

---

## Technique 1: Spaced repetition

**What it is:** Review words at increasing intervals.

**Why it works:** Your brain strengthens memories each time you recall them just before forgetting.

**How to do it:**

| Day | Action |
|-----|--------|
| Day 1 | Learn 10 new words |
| Day 2 | Review Day 1 + learn 10 new |
| Day 4 | Review Day 1 & 2 |
| Day 7 | Review all from Days 1-4 |
| Day 14 | Review all |
| Day 30 | Final review |

**Phone method (no app needed):**
1. Create a note called "English Words — Week 1"
2. Write each word with example
3. Set reminders: Day 2, Day 4, Day 7, Day 14
4. When reminder pops up, quiz yourself before looking

## Technique 2: The keyword method (for Dari speakers)

**What it is:** Connect English word to a similar-sounding Dari word, then create a mental image.

**Example:**

**English word:** "bread"  
**Sounds like Dari:** "برید" (berid — doesn't mean bread, but sounds similar)  
**Mental image:** Picture cutting (برید) bread  
**Result:** When you hear "bread," you think of cutting → bread

**More examples:**

| English | Sounds like (Dari/Hazaragi) | Image |
|---------|---------------------------|-------|
| name | نام | Direct match! |
| mother | مادر (mādar) | Direct match! |
| sister | سیستر (sounds similar) | Your sister |
| time | تایم (borrowed word) | Clock time |

**For words that don't sound similar:** Create an image with meaning.

**English:** "hope"  
**Dari meaning:** امید (omid)  
**Image:** Picture yourself with a bright light (symbol of hope/omid)

> **Dari tip:** Many English words are borrowed into Dari (computer, internet, telephone, etc.). You already know these — just practice English pronunciation!

## Technique 3: Word families (learn groups, not singles)

**What it is:** Learn related words together.

**Example family: "study"**

| Word | Type | Example |
|------|------|---------|
| study | verb | I **study** English. |
| student | noun (person) | I am a **student**. |
| studious | adjective | She is a **studious** person. |
| studies | noun (subjects) | I love language **studies**. |

**When you learn one word, learn its whole family** — your brain stores them as a connected network.

**More families:**

- teach → teacher → taught → teaching
- learn → learner → learned → learning → lesson
- work → worker → worked → working
- write → writer → wrote → written → writing

---

## Technique 4: Mnemonics (memory tricks)

**What it is:** Create a story or phrase to remember spelling or meaning.

**Example: Remember "necessary"**
- "One **c**ollar, two **s**leeves" → ne**c**e**ss**ary

**Example: Remember "because"**
- **B**ig **E**lephants **C**an **A**lways **U**nderstand **S**mall **E**lephants → BECAUSE

**Create your own:**
- Make them funny or personal
- The weirder the story, the better you'll remember

---

## Technique 5: Visualization (mental images)

**What it is:** Create a vivid mental picture for each word.

**Example: "hope"**
- Close your eyes
- Picture a sunrise after a long night
- Feel the warmth
- Connect: hope = sunrise = new beginning

**Example: "courage"**
- Picture yourself standing strong
- Feel your heart beating
- See yourself taking a difficult step forward

**For abstract words** (like freedom, love, hope), connect them to:
- A personal memory
- A symbol (dove = peace, heart = love, sunrise = hope)
- A person who represents it

---

## Technique 6: Personalization (make it about YOUR life)

**What it is:** Use new words to describe your own life.

**Don't:** "The student studies English."  
**Do:** "I study English every morning in my quiet room."

**Example set:**

| Word | Generic sentence (forget easily) | Personal sentence (remember better) |
|------|----------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| tired | She is tired. | I am tired after studying for two hours. |
| happy | He is happy. | I feel happy when I learn a new word. |
| hope | Hope is important. | I have hope for my future education. |

**Your brain remembers personal experiences better than abstract facts.**

---

## Technique 7: Collocations (words that go together)

**What it is:** Learn words in their natural partnerships.

**Don't learn:** make, do, take, give (separately)  
**Do learn:**

- **make** a mistake, a decision, a plan, progress, a difference
- **do** homework, exercises, your best, the dishes, research
- **take** a test, a break, time, a photo, notes
- **give** advice, an answer, a speech, permission, support

**When you learn a verb, write 3-5 nouns it commonly pairs with.**

---

## Technique 8: The memory palace (for advanced learners)

**What it is:** Associate words with locations in a familiar place (your house).

**How:**
1. Choose a familiar route in your home (door → hallway → kitchen → bedroom)
2. Place vocabulary words at each location with a vivid image
3. When you need to recall, mentally walk through your house

**Example:**
- **Door:** "open" — picture the door opening with golden light
- **Hallway:** "walk" — see yourself walking slowly
- **Kitchen:** "cook" — smell the bread cooking
- **Bedroom:** "sleep" — see yourself resting peacefully

**This technique is powerful but takes practice. Try it once you've mastered simpler methods.**

---

## Technique 9: Active recall (test yourself)

**What it is:** Quiz yourself BEFORE looking at answers.

**Don't:** Read word list 10 times  
**Do:** Read once, then cover the answers and try to recall

**Method:**
1. Write English word on one line
2. Write Dari + example on next line
3. **Cover the answer**
4. Try to recall meaning
5. Check
6. If wrong → mark for review tomorrow

**Research shows:** Testing yourself is 3x more effective than passive review.

---

## Technique 10: Emotional connection

**What it is:** Connect words to emotions or important moments.

**Example:** You learn the word "courage" (جرات) on a day when you do something brave (like starting English lessons despite fear).

**Every time you see "courage," you'll remember:** "This is the word I learned the day I started my journey."

**Strong emotions = strong memories.**

---

## Daily vocabulary routine (10 minutes)

**Morning (3 min):** Review yesterday's words (active recall — cover answers)  
**Midday (4 min):** Learn 10 new words with personal sentences  
**Evening (3 min):** Quiz yourself on all new words from today

---

## Common memory mistakes

| Mistake | Why it fails | Better approach |
|---------|--------------|-----------------|
| Reading lists 10 times | Passive, no mental effort | Test yourself (active recall) |
| Learning 50 words in one day | Overwhelms memory | 10-15 words per day, spaced out |
| No review | Memory fades | Review Day 2, 4, 7, 14, 30 |
| Only translating | No context or connection | Use in personal sentences |
| Silent study only | No multi-sensory input | Say aloud, write, visualize |

## Memory helpers

### Use all senses:
- **See** the word written
- **Hear** the word spoken (record yourself)
- **Say** the word aloud
- **Write** the word
- **Feel** the meaning (visualize)

### Create word webs:

```
         study
        /  |  \
    student  studies  studio
       |
    studious
```

### Use colors:
- Verbs in **blue**
- Nouns in **green**
- Adjectives in **red**

Visual organization helps memory.

## Tracking what works for YOU

After 2 weeks, check:
- Which technique helps you remember best?
- Which words stick easily? (Why?)
- Which words keep slipping? (Try a different technique)

**Everyone's brain is different.** Find what works for you, then use that method consistently.

---

## Long-term maintenance

### Monthly review:
- First Sunday of month: Review 100 random words
- Use them in a 200-word essay

### When you forget a word:
- Don't feel bad
- Learn it again (this time it sticks better)
- Use it 5 times in the next 2 days

**Forgetting is normal. Relearning is faster.**
