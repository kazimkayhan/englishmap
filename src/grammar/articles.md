# Articles (a, an, the)

**Learning goal:** Use a, an, and the correctly in sentences

---

## Why articles matter

Articles are small words that come before nouns. They seem simple, but they change the meaning:

- "I need **a** phone." (any phone)
- "I need **the** phone." (a specific phone we both know about)

English uses articles constantly. Dari doesn't have articles like this, so they need focused practice.

> **Key fact:** This is one of the hardest parts of English for Dari speakers. Be patient with yourself — it takes time.

---

## The three articles

| Article | When to use | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| **a** | Before singular noun starting with consonant sound | **a** book, **a** phone |
| **an** | Before singular noun starting with vowel sound | **an** apple, **an** hour |
| **the** | Specific noun (both speaker and listener know which one) | **the** book (we both know which book) |

---

## Indefinite articles: a / an

### When to use "a" or "an"

Use **a / an** when:
1. You mention something **for the first time**
2. You mean **any one** of that thing (not a specific one)
3. The noun is **singular** and **countable**

**Choose "a" or "an" based on SOUND, not spelling:**

| Article | Used before | Examples |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| **a** | Consonant sound | **a** book, **a** phone, **a** university (sounds like YOO) |
| **an** | Vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u sound) | **an** apple, **an** hour (silent h), **an** orange |

---

### Examples with "a" / "an"

**First mention:**
- I need **a** pen. (any pen)
- She has **a** phone. (one phone, not specific)
- I want **an** apple. (any apple)

**One of many:**
- **A** student asked a question. (one student out of many)
- I have **a** sister. (I might have more than one)
- She is **a** teacher. (her profession)

**Jobs and professions:**
- I am **a** student.
- She is **a** doctor.
- He is **an** engineer.

> **Dari tip:** In Dari, you say "من معلم هستم" (I teacher am) with no article. In English, you MUST say "I am **a** teacher."

---

## Definite article: the

### When to use "the"

Use **the** when:
1. **Both** you and the listener know which specific thing you're talking about
2. There is only **one** of something
3. You mentioned it **before** (second mention)

**"The" works with:**
- Singular nouns: **the** book
- Plural nouns: **the** books
- Uncountable nouns: **the** water

---

### Examples with "the"

**Second mention (already introduced):**
- I bought **a** book. **The** book is very good.
- She has **a** phone. **The** phone is old.

**Only one exists (in context):**
- **The** sun is bright. (only one sun)
- Please close **the** door. (the door of this room)
- **The** internet is slow today. (the one we both use)

**Specific, known to both people:**
- Can you give me **the** notebook? (we both know which notebook)
- **The** teacher is kind. (we both know who the teacher is)

**Superlatives and unique things:**
- She is **the** best student. (superlative)
- **The** first word is hard. (order)

---

## When NOT to use articles (zero article)

Don't use **a/an/the** in these cases:

### 1. Plural or uncountable nouns (general meaning)

- **Books** are useful. (books in general, not specific books)
- **Water** is important. (water in general)
- **Students** study English. (students in general)

**But:** Use "the" if you mean specific ones:
- **The books** on the table are mine. (specific books)
- **The water** in this cup is cold. (specific water)

---

### 2. Names of people and places (usually)

- **Sara** is my friend. (not **a** Sara or **the** Sara)
- I live in **Kabul**. (not **the** Kabul)
- I study **English**. (not **an** English or **the** English)

**Exception:** Some country names use "the":
- **the** United States, **the** United Kingdom, **the** Netherlands

---

### 3. Abstract nouns (general concepts)

- **Love** is powerful. (love in general)
- **Freedom** is important.
- **Education** changes lives.

**But:** Use "the" if you mean a specific instance:
- **The love** she shows her family is beautiful. (specific love)

---

### 4. Meals (general)

- I eat **breakfast** at 7 AM.
- She has **lunch** at noon.

**But:** Use "the" if specific:
- **The breakfast** we had yesterday was delicious.

---

## Common patterns

### Jobs and professions

- I am **a** student.
- She is **an** engineer.
- He is **a** teacher.

### Time expressions

- once **a** week
- twice **a** day
- $10 **an** hour

### Unique things

- **the** sun, **the** moon, **the** earth
- **the** sky, **the** sea, **the** ground

### Directions and places in town

- at **the** door, on **the** floor, in **the** corner
- at **the** market, at **the** hospital (specific one)

But:
- at **home**, at **school**, at **work** (no article)

> **Hazaragi tip:** Even if articles don't exist in Hazaragi, think of "the" as meaning "that specific one" — it will help you remember when to use it.

## Practice activities

### Activity 1: Choose a or an
1. ___ book (a)
2. ___ apple (an)
3. ___ hour (an)
4. ___ university (a — sounds like YOO)
5. ___ orange (an)

### Activity 2: Fill in a, an, or the
1. I have ___ phone. ___ phone is old. (a / The)
2. She is ___ teacher. (a)
3. ___ sun is bright. (The)
4. I need ___ apple and ___ banana. (an / a)
5. Can you close ___ door? (the)

### Activity 3: Add articles where needed (or write "no article")
1. ___ Students study English. (no article — general)
2. I live in ___ Kabul. (no article — city name)
3. I need ___ help. (no article — abstract/uncountable, unless you mean "the help you promised")
4. She has ___ sister. (a)
5. ___ Water is important. (no article — general)

---

## Self-check quiz

1. Complete: "I am ___ student." (a)
2. Complete: "She has ___ old phone. ___ phone doesn't work." (an / The)
3. When do you use "an" instead of "a"? *(Before vowel sound)*
4. Fix error: "I like the books." (when meaning books in general) *(I like books.)*
5. Do you say "the Kabul" or "Kabul"? *(Kabul — no article for city names)*

## Common mistakes (Dari speakers)

| Mistake | Why wrong | Correct |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| I am student. | Missing article for profession | I am **a** student. |
| I need the help. | "Help" is general here | I need help. |
| The books are good. | Meaning books in general | Books are good. |
| I have phone. | Missing article for countable singular | I have **a** phone. |
| I live in the Kabul. | City names don't use "the" | I live in Kabul. |
| She is teacher. | Missing article | She is **a** teacher. |

---

## Summary: Quick decision tree

**Ask yourself:**

1. **Is it countable and singular?**
   - Yes → Use **a/an** or **the**
   - No (plural or uncountable) → Usually no article (unless specific)

2. **Is it the first time mentioning it?**
   - Yes → Use **a/an**
   - No (we talked about it before) → Use **the**

3. **Do both people know which one?**
   - Yes → Use **the**
   - No → Use **a/an**

4. **Is it a general statement?**
   - Yes → No article (Books are good.)
   - No → Use **the** (The books on the table are good.)

## Daily practice plan

**Day 1:** Write 10 sentences with "a"  
**Day 2:** Write 10 sentences with "an"  
**Day 3:** Write 10 sentences with "the" (second mention)  
**Day 4:** Write 10 sentences with no article (general plurals)  
**Day 5:** Mix all — write 15 sentences using a/an/the/no article  
**Day 6:** Take a paragraph and underline every article; explain why it's there  
**Day 7:** Write 1 paragraph about your day (10 sentences) — check articles carefully

---

## Don't worry about perfection

**Important message:** Articles are difficult. Even advanced learners make mistakes. Native speakers will understand you even if you miss an article.

**Priority:**
1. First, focus on **a/an** for jobs: "I am a student."
2. Then, focus on **the** for second mentions.
3. Over time, you'll develop a feeling for when to use them.
