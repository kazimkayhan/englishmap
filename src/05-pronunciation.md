# Pronunciation (American English)

**Stage 1 → Stage 4** · Daily 5–10 min alongside other skills

---

## Learning goals

1. Hear and produce **American vowels** (ship vs sheep)
2. Pronounce **th**, **r**, and **w** more clearly
3. Use **word stress** so listeners understand you
4. Fix **Dari-influenced** sound habits
5. Use your phone to **record and compare**

---

## Why this skill matters

Good grammar with unclear sounds still blocks communication. Clear pronunciation builds **confidence** — people listen to your ideas, not only your accent.

> *Goal: understood, not perfect. Native-like accent is optional.*

---

## Level focus

**Stage 1** starts; refine through **Stage 4**.

---

## Step-by-step lessons

### Lesson 1 — American English basics (15 min)

- **R** is stronger than in British English: *water*, *far*
- **T** between vowels sounds like quick *d* in casual speech: *water*, *better* (later stage)
- Final consonants: try to **finish** the sound: *help*, not *hel*

---

### Lesson 2 — Vowel pairs (20 min)

| Sound | Example | Common mistake |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| /ɪ/ short i | **sh**i**p** | sounds like sheep |
| /iː/ long ee | sh**ee**p | |
| /ɛ/ | b**e**d | |
| /æ/ | c**a**t | |
| /ʌ/ | c**u**p | |
| /ɑː/ | f**a**ther | |

**Practice sentence:**  
*I sit in a big city and drink tea.*

Say slowly. Record. Listen.

---

### Lesson 3 — Consonants for Dari speakers (20 min)

**TH (voiceless)** — *thank, think, three*  
- Tongue lightly between teeth, air out  
- Not *tank* or *sank*

**TH (voiced)** — *this, that, mother*  
- Tongue between teeth, voice on

**P vs F** — *pat* vs *fat* — lips vs teeth

**V vs W** — *very* vs *west* — teeth on lip for *v*

**H** — *he, home* — breathe out, not silent

> **Dari note:** Sounds that do not exist in Dari need **daily 2-minute drills**. Short daily beats long weekly.

---

### Lesson 4 — Word stress (15 min)

English stressed syllable is **louder and longer**.

| Word | Stress (CAPS) |
|------|---------------|
| PHOtograph | pho-TO-graphy (shift!) |
| comPUter | |
| eduCAtion | |
| paTIENT | |

**Rule:** Stress wrong syllable → word sounds foreign or unclear.

**Practice:** Clap on stressed syllable: com-**PU**-ter

---

### Lesson 5 — Sentence rhythm (15 min)

Content words (nouns, verbs) stronger; function words (*a, the, to*) softer.

- I **want** to **study** **English**.
- She **lives** at **home**.

Read aloud with exaggeration first, then natural speed.

---

### Lesson 6 — Phone recording routine (10 min daily)

1. Read 3 sentences from any module  
2. Record voice memo  
3. Listen — note one sound to fix tomorrow  
4. Optional: compare to slow American audio (see [15-resources.md](15-resources.md))  
5. **Privacy:** store in locked/private folder; delete monthly

## Examples

**Minimal pairs (train your ear):**

| A | B |
|---|---|
| ship | sheep |
| live (verb) | leave |
| very | berry (different vowels) |

**Introduction with clear stress:**

> **HEL**-lo. **MY** **NAME** **IS** Zahra. I **STUD**-y **ENG**-lish at **HOME**.

Then make it natural without losing key stresses.

## Self-check questions

1. What is the difference between *ship* and *sheep*?
2. Where is your tongue for *thank*?
3. Why does word stress matter?
4. How can you practice safely on a shared phone?
5. Is perfect accent required for good communication?

---

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Replacing *th* with *t* or *s* | Slow drill 20 reps |
| Dropping final consonants | Exaggerate final sound 10 times |
| Flat stress on all syllables | Clap + CAPS stress |
| Never listening to yourself | Record 3x/week minimum |

## Study habits

- **5 min** before main lesson — one sound only  
- Same sound for **3 days** before switching  
- Praise one improvement per week (not one failure)
