# Dictation Practice

**Learning goal:** Train your ear to catch exact words

---

## What is dictation?

**Dictation** means:
1. Listen to a sentence
2. Write exactly what you hear
3. Check against the correct text

**Why it's powerful:** Dictation forces you to hear EVERY word, including articles (a, the), prepositions (in, on), and verb endings (-s, -ed) that you might miss in casual listening.

---

## Why dictation works

- **Trains precision:** You can't guess — you must hear clearly
- **Reveals blind spots:** Words you always mishear
- **Improves spelling:** You connect sound → spelling
- **Builds focus:** You listen actively, not passively

> **Fact:** 10 minutes of dictation = 30 minutes of passive listening for skill building.

---

## How to do dictation (step-by-step)

### Beginner method (start here)

**Materials:** Any audio with transcript (or this book's dialogues read aloud)

**Steps:**
1. Listen to **one sentence** (5-10 words max)
2. **Pause** audio
3. **Write** what you heard (don't look at text!)
4. **Replay** the sentence (1-2 more times if needed)
5. **Check** against the transcript
6. **Circle** mistakes
7. **Practice** the words you missed
8. **Move to next sentence**

**Time:** 10-15 minutes (5-10 sentences)

---

### Intermediate method

**Same as above, but:**
- Longer sentences (10-15 words)
- Faster audio (natural speed)
- Listen only TWICE before writing (no more replays)

---

### Advanced method

**Paragraph dictation:**
1. Listen to entire paragraph (30-60 seconds)
2. Write everything you can remember
3. Listen again, fill in gaps
4. Check transcript

## Sample dictation sentences (by level)

### Level 1 (Beginner — slow, clear)

1. "I study English every day."
2. "She has a book and a pen."
3. "We live in a small house."
4. "My mother works at home."
5. "I want to learn new words."

**Check for:**
- Articles (a, the)
- Verb endings (-s)
- Capitalization
- Punctuation

---

### Level 2 (Intermediate — natural speed)

1. "I decided to start learning English because I want a better future."
2. "Every morning, I wake up at six and study for thirty minutes."
3. "Although it's difficult, I don't give up."
4. "My sister and I practice speaking together."
5. "I believe that education can change lives."

**Check for:**
- Past tense endings (-ed)
- Connecting words (because, although)
- Contractions (it's, don't)

---

### Level 3 (Advanced)

1. "Despite facing numerous challenges, she continued pursuing her education with unwavering determination."
2. "The ability to communicate effectively in English opens doors to countless opportunities."
3. "Research suggests that consistent daily practice yields better results than intensive weekend sessions."

**Check for:**
- Complex vocab
- Long sentences
- Formal grammar structures

## Common dictation errors (and fixes)

| Error | Example | Why | Fix |
|-------|---------|-----|-----|
| Missing articles | "I have book" | Dari doesn't use articles | Focus listening on "a/an/the" |
| Wrong verb ending | "She study" | Missed -s | Listen for word endings carefully |
| Homophones | "their" vs "there" | Same sound, different spelling | Check context, learn common pairs |
| Missing words | "I want learn" | Didn't hear "to" | Listen for small function words |
| Wrong tense | "I study yesterday" | Didn't hear "-ed" | Focus on verb endings |

## How to find audio with transcripts

**Method 1: YouTube**
- Search "[topic] English with subtitles"
- Turn on CC (closed captions)
- Use manual subtitles if available (auto-generated are less accurate)

**Method 2: Podcast apps**
- Many podcasts offer transcripts on their websites
- Search "podcast with transcript"

**Method 3: Use this book**
- Read dialogues from [Beginner Foundations](../02-beginner-foundations.md), [Grammar](../03-core-grammar.md), etc.
- Have someone read them aloud, or use text-to-speech
- Write what you hear, then check against the book

## Self-check quiz

After 2 weeks of dictation:
1. Are you hearing articles (a, an, the) more often? ✅ / ❌
2. Are you catching verb endings (-s, -ed, -ing)? ✅ / ❌
3. Can you spell most common words correctly? ✅ / ❌
4. Is your accuracy improving? (fewer mistakes) ✅ / ❌

If mostly ✅ → keep going, maybe increase difficulty  
If mostly ❌ → slow down audio, use easier sentences

## Dictation tips

1. **Don't look at the text first** — that defeats the purpose
2. **Write as you hear** — even if you think it's wrong
3. **Use pencil** (or type) — easier to fix mistakes
4. **Focus on one problem** — if you always miss articles, listen ONLY for articles this week
5. **Replay is okay** — beginners can replay 3-5 times
6. **Check after writing** — don't check while writing

---

## Combining dictation with other skills

**Dictation → Pronunciation:**
- After checking, read the sentence aloud 5 times
- Mimic the speaker's rhythm and stress

**Dictation → Grammar:**
- After checking, identify grammar patterns
- Example: "She **studies** English" — present simple with -s

**Dictation → Vocabulary:**
- Keep a log of words you couldn't spell
- Add them to your vocabulary list

## Advanced challenge: Self-dictation

**No audio needed:**
1. Read a paragraph silently
2. Close the book
3. Write the paragraph from memory
4. Check

**This tests:** Reading comprehension + spelling + memory

---

## Common questions

**Q: How many times can I replay?**  
A: Beginners: 3-5 times. Intermediate: 2-3 times. Advanced: 1-2 times.

**Q: Should I pause after every word?**  
A: No. Listen to the full sentence, then write. This trains your short-term memory.

**Q: What if I can't understand at all?**  
A: The material is too hard. Choose an easier source.

**Q: Is it okay to use slow-speed audio?**  
A: Yes! Start slow, gradually increase speed.
