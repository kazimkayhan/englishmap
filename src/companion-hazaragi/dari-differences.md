# Hazaragi vs Dari: Key Differences

**Understanding the relationship between Hazaragi, Standard Dari, and your English learning**

---

## Overview

Hazaragi and Dari are closely related but distinct language varieties within the Persian language family. Understanding the differences helps you:

1. Read Dari-script materials while thinking in Hazaragi
2. Avoid confusion when using Dari resources
3. Feel confident that Hazaragi is valid, not "wrong Dari"

---

## The Relationship

```
Persian Language Family
    ├── Dari (Standard Afghan Persian)
    ├── Farsi (Iranian Persian)
    └── Tajik (Tajikistan Persian)
        └── Hazaragi (Distinct dialect, closest to Dari)
```

**Hazaragi** is sometimes called:
- A dialect of Dari
- A separate language
- Hazara Dari
- *Azaragi* (older term)

**What matters:** It's your first language, and it's valid.

## Key Linguistic Differences

### 1. Phonology (Sounds)

| Feature | Hazaragi | Standard Dari |
|---------|----------|---------------|
| **Vowel reduction** | Common (*āftāb* → *aftow*) | Less reduction |
| **Final *-ow* / *-aw*** | Very common (*daryow*, *aftow*) | Final *-ā* or *-āb* (*daryā*, *āftāb*) |
| **Vowel harmony** | Some patterns (regional) | Less systematic |
| **Consonant clusters** | Simplification common | More consonant clusters retained |
| **Stress** | Often initial or penultimate | More varied |

**Example:**
- Dari: *āftāb* (sun) → Hazaragi: *aftow*
- Dari: *daryā* (river) → Hazaragi: *daryow*

### 2. Vocabulary

#### Common Hazaragi Words Different from Dari

| Concept | Hazaragi | Standard Dari | English |
|---------|----------|---------------|---------|
| House | **کور** *kor* | خانه *khāna* | house |
| Morning | **سحر** *sahar* | صبح *sobh* | morning |
| People | **مردیم** *mardim* | مردم *mardom* | people |
| Sun | **آفتو** *aftow* | آفتاب *āftāb* | sun |
| Book | **کتو** *ketow* | کتاب *ketāb* | book |

#### Shared Vocabulary

Most everyday words are the **same or very similar**:
- *zan* (woman)
- *mard* (man)
- *non* (bread)
- *āw* (water)
- *ketāb* or *ketow* (book) — regional variation

### 3. Grammar

#### Verb Conjugation Differences

**Example: "to go"**

| Person | Hazaragi (Present) | Dari (Present) |
|--------|-------------------|----------------|
| I go | **میریم** *mī-berem* | می‌روم *mī-rawam* |
| You go | **میری** *mī-berī* | می‌روی *mī-rawī* |
| He/She goes | **میره** *mī-bera* | می‌رود *mī-rawad* |

**Hazaragi simplifies** the conjugation pattern.

#### Pronoun Dropping

Both Hazaragi and Dari allow **pro-drop** (omitting subject pronouns), but Hazaragi does it more frequently:

**Hazaragi:**
> *Mī-berem.* ("Am going" — no "I")

**Dari:**
> *(Man) mī-rawam.* ("I am going" — optional "I")

**English:**
> *"I am going."* (Subject required!)

### 4. Syntax (Word Order)

Both Hazaragi and Dari use **SOV** (Subject-Object-Verb):

**Hazaragi & Dari:**
> *Man ketow mī-khōnam.*
> (I book read.)

**English uses SVO:**
> *I read a book.*
> (I read book.)

**For English learners:** You must switch your word order!

## False Friends (Words That Look Similar But Differ)

| Word | Hazaragi Meaning | Dari Meaning | English |
|------|-----------------|--------------|---------|
| **شو** *shaw* | Night | (Different: *shab*) | Night |
| **سحر** *sahar* | Morning | (Different: *sobh*) | Morning |
| **کور** *kor* | House | Blind (in Dari, *kor* = blind) | House |

**Watch out for:** *kor* — in Dari it means "blind," but in Hazaragi it means "house"!

## When to Use Dari vs Hazaragi

### Use Hazaragi

- ✅ Thinking and speaking at home
- ✅ Talking to Hazara community
- ✅ Internal monologue while learning English
- ✅ Connecting with your identity and culture

### Use Dari

- ✅ Reading Afghan government documents (if needed)
- ✅ Reading books/materials in Perso-Arabic script (most are Standard Dari)
- ✅ Communicating formally in Afghanistan (Dari is official language)
- ✅ Using Dari companion guides in this book (grammar explanations)

### Use Both

- ✅ Daily life in mixed communities (code-switching is normal)
- ✅ Learning English (use whichever language helps you understand better)

## Code-Switching Strategies

**Code-switching** = alternating between languages in conversation.

Many Hazara people switch between:
- Hazaragi (home, community)
- Dari (formal, written, official)
- Pashto (if in mixed areas)
- English (learning/professional)
- Urdu (if in Pakistan/diaspora)

**This is a superpower, not a weakness.**

### Example of Natural Code-Switching

> *Man dar kor-am English mī-khōnam. Har rōz vocabulary words mī-nivisam. It's difficult, amma mī-khām یاد بگیرم.*

Translation:
> "I study English at home. Every day I write vocabulary words. It's difficult, but I want to learn."

(Hazaragi + English + Dari mix — **completely normal!**)

## Hazaragi Pride & Dari Respect

### Hazaragi Is Valid

- Hazaragi is **not** broken, incorrect, or inferior Dari
- It's a natural linguistic variety with systematic patterns
- Millions of people speak it
- Oral literature, songs, and stories exist in Hazaragi
- Your children can learn both Hazaragi and English

### Dari Is Useful

- Written materials in Afghanistan are mostly Dari
- Learning Dari alongside Hazaragi is practical
- English materials sometimes use Dari as L1 reference
- Knowing both = more resources available

### English Is Separate

- Don't think of English as "Dari with different words"
- English grammar is **fundamentally different**:
  - SVO word order
  - Articles required
  - Plural forms strict
  - Tense system different
- Learn English **directly**, not through Dari

## Practical Tips for Learning English as a Hazaragi Speaker

### 1. Think in Hazaragi, Learn in English

**Don't do this:**
> Hazaragi → Dari → English (too many steps!)

**Do this:**
> Hazaragi → English (direct association)

**Example:**
- See a table → think *mēz* (Hazaragi) → say *"table"* (English)

### 2. Use Dari Resources When Helpful

**This book has both Hazaragi and Dari companions.**

- **Hazaragi guide:** Pronunciation, daily vocabulary
- **Dari guide:** Grammar explanations (more formal/written)

Use **both** as needed!

### 3. Don't Correct Your Hazaragi

Some Hazaragi speakers feel pressure to "fix" their Hazaragi to sound more like Dari.

**For English learning, this doesn't matter.**

- Your goal: Hazaragi → English (fluent)
- Not: Hazaragi → Dari → English (unnecessary)

### 4. Record Yourself in Hazaragi First

Before recording English practice, record yourself speaking in Hazaragi:

> *Name-am [name] ast. Man engelīsī mī-khām یاد بگیرم.*

Then in English:

> *My name is [name]. I want to learn English.*

**Why?** You'll see that accent = normal. Every language has its own sounds.

## Summary Table: Hazaragi vs Dari vs English

| Feature | Hazaragi | Dari | English |
|---------|----------|------|---------|
| **Word order** | SOV | SOV | SVO |
| **Articles** | None | None | *a, an, the* (required) |
| **Vowels** | ~4-5 | ~6-7 | ~12-15 |
| **Pronouns dropped** | Very common | Common | Never (subject required) |
| **Verb conjugation** | Simpler | More complex | Different system |
| **Written form** | Perso-Arabic (unofficial) | Perso-Arabic (official) | Latin alphabet |
| **Script direction** | RTL (right-to-left) | RTL | LTR (left-to-right) |

## Common Questions

### Q: Should I learn Dari before English?

**A:** No! You can learn English directly from Hazaragi. This guide helps you do that.

### Q: Will learning English make me forget Hazaragi?

**A:** No. Bilingualism/multilingualism strengthens your brain. Keep speaking Hazaragi at home.

### Q: If I can't spell Hazaragi correctly, how can I learn English?

**A:** Hazaragi has no standardized spelling. English **does** have standardized spelling — you'll learn it step by step in this book. Totally separate skills.

### Q: Is Hazaragi a "dialect" or a "language"?

**A:** Linguistically, it's a dialect of Persian. **Socially and politically**, language vs dialect debates are complex. **What matters:** Hazaragi is your valid first language. Treat it with pride while learning others.
