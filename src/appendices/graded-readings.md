## Reading 1 — Level A (Beginner)

**My Name**

Hello. My name is Amina. I am twenty years old. I live in Afghanistan. I study English at home. I use my phone. I learn ten words every day. I want to read books in English. I am patient. Thank you.

**Questions:** 1. Name? 2. Age? 3. Where live? 4. How many words daily? 5. What does she want?

## Reading 2 — Level B (Elementary)

**A Message to My Sister**

Dear sister,

Good morning. I am fine today. I studied English for thirty minutes. I learned the words *hope*, *courage*, and *practice*. Tomorrow I will listen to a slow podcast. Please study with me on Saturday. We can read this text together.

Love,
Maryam

**Questions:** 1. How long did she study? 2. Name three words. 3. What will she do tomorrow? 4. When does she want to study together? 5. Who is the message for?

## Reading 3 — Level C (Pre-intermediate)

**Studying When Life Is Hard**

Last year, Fatima stopped school for several months. She felt sad, but she did not quit English. She studied at home every day when she had time. Sometimes she studied only fifteen minutes. She used free websites and downloaded audio files.

Now Fatima can write a paragraph about her day. She believes small steps are stronger than one long day per month. Her mother is proud. Fatima wants to take an online course next year.

**Questions:** 1. Did she quit English? 2. How long on bad days? 3. What can she do now? 4. Who is proud? 5. What is her plan for next year?

## Reading 4 — Level D (Intermediate)

**Remote Work and Language**

Many young women learn English because they want remote work. Remote jobs need clear writing and calm video calls. You do not need perfect grammar on day one, but you need consistent practice.

First, build a daily habit. Second, record your voice and listen. Third, write short emails to yourself. Fourth, read news in easy English. Finally, apply for small tasks when you feel ready.

However, protect your privacy online. Do not share your address. Use trusted platforms. If a job offer looks too good, it may be a scam.

**Questions:** 1. Why learn English for work? 2. List two skills for remote jobs. 3. What are four steps in the text? 4. What privacy warning is given? 5. What is the main idea?

## Reading 5 — Level E (Upper intermediate)

**Education and Hope**

Education is not only a building with desks. It is also a lamp in a dark room. When classrooms close, learning can continue on a phone, in a notebook, or in a sister circle at home.

Researchers often report that literacy improves health and income over time. Although resources are limited, women who read and write gain more control over information. They can verify news, understand medicine labels, and support their children.

Therefore, self-study is not a small choice. It is an act of hope. Every word you learn today may help you tomorrow — and the next year, and the next decade.

**Questions:** 1. Metaphor: education is a ___? 2. Name two benefits of literacy from the text. 3. What can women verify? 4. What is self-study compared to? 5. Write one sentence agreeing or disagreeing.

## Reading 6 — Level B (Health)

**A Simple Day**

Sara wakes up at six. She drinks warm water and tea. She does not feel well, so she rests for one hour. Later she studies English for twenty minutes. She eats rice and vegetables for lunch. In the evening, she walks in the house and stretches her arms. She goes to bed early. She says, "Rest is also study for the body."

**Questions:** 1. What time does she wake up? 2. Why does she rest one hour? 3. How long does she study? 4. What does she eat? 5. What is her quote about rest?

## Reading 7 — Level C (Technology)

**My Phone Is My Classroom**

When classrooms are far or closed, my phone becomes my classroom. I download lessons on good Wi-Fi days. I save vocabulary in my notes app. I record my voice and listen again. Sometimes the battery is low, so I plan charging time like I plan study time. I do not open unsafe links. I use passwords my family does not know. English on my phone is freedom with responsibility.

**Questions:** 1. What is her classroom? 2. When does she download? 3. Name two study actions on phone. 4. Two safety habits? 5. Main idea in one sentence?

---

## Reading 8 — Level D (Opinion)

**Should Women Study Alone at Home?**

Some people say women should not study alone. I disagree. Home study can be safe and strong when the family supports education. A woman can learn grammar, listen to podcasts, and write emails without traveling. However, she should protect her privacy online and choose trusted apps.

In my opinion, the question is not "alone or not alone." The question is: does she have time, safety, and hope? If yes, home study can change her future.

**Questions:** 1. What do some people say? 2. Does the writer agree? 3. Name two skills learned at home. 4. One online safety rule? 5. What is the writer's real question?

## Reading 9 — Level D (Narrative past)

**The Day I Spoke English**

Last Friday I was nervous. I opened my notebook and read six sentences aloud. My sister listened from the kitchen. At first I forgot a word, but I continued. I said, "I study because I want a better future." My sister smiled and said, "Good job." That small moment was my victory. I recorded it on my phone. I will listen again when I feel afraid.

**Questions:** 1. When? 2. Who listened? 3. What problem happened? 4. What sentence did she say? 5. Why will she listen to the recording?

---

## Reading 10 — Level E (Argument)

**Language as a Tool, Not a Trophy**

Fluent accents attract attention on social media, but education is not a performance. For many Afghan women, English is a **tool**: to read health information, to apply for scholarships, to work online, and to teach younger sisters.

Although perfect pronunciation is admirable, **communication** matters more. Learners should measure progress by tasks they can do today — write an email, understand a headline, speak for two minutes — not by comparison with native speakers.

Therefore, policy and families should support daily practice, safe internet use, and patience. Language opens doors only when the learner keeps walking.

**Questions:** 1. English is a tool for what four things? 2. What matters more than perfect accent? 3. Give two progress measures. 4. What should families support? 5. Write 2-sentence summary.
