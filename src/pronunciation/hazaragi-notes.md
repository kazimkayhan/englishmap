# Hazaragi Accent Notes

**Learning goal:** Understand how Hazaragi affects your English pronunciation

---

## Why this page exists

Most English learning materials for Afghan learners focus on standard Dari (Kabuli Dari). **But if Hazaragi is your first language**, some pronunciation patterns will be different.

This page:
- Recognizes Hazaragi as a distinct language variety
- Points out specific Hazaragi-English transfer patterns
- Helps you leverage what's already similar to English
- Identifies what needs extra practice

> **Important:** Your Hazaragi background is a **strength**, not a problem. Some Hazaragi sounds are actually closer to English than standard Dari sounds are.

---

## Hazaragi vs Dari: Key sound differences

### Difference 1: Vowel system

Hazaragi has some vowel distinctions that Kabuli Dari doesn't:
- Hazaragi vowels may be longer or pronounced differently in some contexts
- This sometimes makes English vowel length distinctions (ship/sheep) **easier** for Hazaragi speakers

**Your advantage:** If you're already used to distinguishing vowel length in Hazaragi, English /ɪ/ vs /iː/ may feel more natural.

---

### Difference 2: /o/ and /u/ sounds

Hazaragi preserves different pronunciations of "و" in some words compared to Kabuli Dari:
- Kabuli Dari: often /u/
- Hazaragi: sometimes /o/

**For English:** Practice both /oʊ/ (go, home) and /uː/ (food, blue) carefully. Don't let Hazaragi patterns interfere with English distinctions.

---

### Difference 3: Retroflex sounds

Hazaragi has some retroflex consonants (tongue curled back) in certain contexts, influenced by contact with other languages.

**For English:** American English /r/ is retroflex (tongue pulled back). Hazaragi speakers **may find English /r/ easier** than Kabuli Dari speakers do.

**Practice:**
- car, very, water, mother, first, word

If English /r/ feels natural, use it! If not, practice the retroflex tongue position.

---

### Difference 4: Consonant clusters

Hazaragi allows some consonant combinations, but like standard Dari, struggles with English initial clusters (st-, sp-, str-).

**Common Hazaragi speaker mistake:**
- "study" → adding a vowel before: "estudy"

**Fix:** Same as for Dari speakers — practice starting directly with the cluster.

**Drill:** study, speak, street, strong, special (×20)

---

## Hazaragi-specific pronunciation tips

### Tip 1: Use your retroflex advantage

If Hazaragi gives you a retroflex /r/ tendency:
- English /r/ (as in "red," "very," "car") is retroflex too
- Practice making your tongue curl back slightly
- This is an **advantage** — many Dari speakers find /r/ difficult

**Test:** Say "water, very, mother" — if it feels natural with a strong /r/, keep doing it!

---

### Tip 2: Watch out for /v/ and /w/

Hazaragi, like Dari, uses "و" for both sounds. But English separates them:
- /v/: teeth on lower lip → very, have, live
- /w/: round lips, no teeth → water, we, what

**Practice pairs:**
| V | W |
|---|---|
| very | wary |
| vest | west |
| vine | wine |

**Daily drill:** "We have very good water."

---

### Tip 3: Final consonants need emphasis

Like standard Dari, Hazaragi often softens or drops final consonants. English requires you to **finish** them clearly:

- stop, test, hand, good, book, students

**Practice holding final sounds for 1 extra second:**
- sto**pppp**, te**ssst**, han**d**

---

### Tip 4: Stress patterns differ from English

Hazaragi stress patterns (which syllable is louder) differ from English. English stress:
- Is stronger (much louder)
- Changes meaning: **RE**-cord (noun) vs re-**CORD** (verb)
- Shifts with suffixes: **PHO**-to-graph → pho-**TO**-gra-phy

**Key practice:** [Word Stress](./stress.md)

**Exaggerate stress for 1 week:**
- **COM**-pu-ter (first syllable VERY LOUD, others soft)
- to-**MOR**-row (second syllable VERY LOUD)

## English sounds that don't exist in Hazaragi (same as Dari)

Even though Hazaragi differs from standard Dari, these English sounds still don't exist in Hazaragi:

### Sound 1: TH /θ/ and /ð/

- /θ/: thank, think, three
- /ð/: this, that, mother

**Tongue position:** Place tongue lightly **between teeth**, blow air out.

**Daily drill (2 minutes):**
- "Thank you for this. I think that is good."

---

### Sound 2: Short /ɪ/ vs long /iː/

Even if Hazaragi vowel length awareness helps, you still need to practice:

| Short /ɪ/ | Long /iː/ |
|-----------|-----------|
| ship | sheep |
| sit | seat |
| it | eat |
| bit | beat |

**Key:** Long vowel is held **2x as long**.

---

### Sound 3: /ɜː/ (bird, word, her)

This sound doesn't exist in Hazaragi or Dari. It's one of the hardest for Afghan learners.

**How to make it:**
1. Relax your mouth (not wide, not closed)
2. Tongue in middle position
3. Make sound from throat: "errr"

**Practice words:** bird, word, her, first, nurse, learn, early, work

## Hazaragi-English sentence intonation

### Observation

Hazaragi sentence melody (intonation) has its own patterns, different from both Kabuli Dari and English.

**English intonation patterns:**
- **Statements:** pitch falls at end
- **Yes/no questions:** pitch rises at end
- **Wh- questions:** pitch falls at end

**Practice:**
- Statement: "I study English." ↘
- Yes/no question: "Do you study English?" ↗
- Wh- question: "Where do you study?" ↘

Record yourself and check: Does your pitch go up or down at the end?

## Spelling and reading notes for Hazaragi speakers

If you learned to read and write Hazaragi in Perso-Arabic script:
- English Latin alphabet is a new system
- Some letters look similar but sound different
- English spelling is **not phonetic** (not 1 letter = 1 sound)

**Examples:**
- "gh" can be silent: "night" (no /g/ sound)
- "th" is one sound: "this" (not /t/ + /h/)
- Same letter, different sounds: "a" in "cat" vs "a" in "made"

**Tip:** Learn English letter **combinations** (digraphs):
- th, sh, ch, ph, wh, gh

**Review:** [The Alphabet](../beginner/alphabet.md)

## Vocabulary notes: Words you already know

Many modern words are borrowed into Hazaragi from English (via Dari or directly):
- تلفن (telephone)
- کمپیوتر (computer)
- انټرنیټ (internet)
- ډاکټر (doctor)

**Your advantage:** You already know these words! Just practice English pronunciation.

**Practice:** Say these words with:
- English stress pattern
- English vowel sounds
- English final consonants

Example:
- Hazaragi: "کمپیوتر" [kompyuter]
- English: "**com**puter" [kəmˈpjutɚ] (stress 1st syllable)

## Regional variations within Hazaragi

Hazaragi has regional variations (Ghazni, Bamyan, Maidan Wardak, Daikundi, etc.). Pronunciation differs slightly by region.

**For English learning:**
- Use your regional Hazaragi as a starting point
- Don't worry if your English accent is different from other Hazaragi speakers' English
- Focus on **clarity** in English, not matching others' accents

---

## Respecting your linguistic identity

**Hazaragi is a language, not a dialect.** It has:
- Its own grammar patterns
- Distinct vocabulary
- Unique phonetic features
- Rich literary and oral traditions

**When learning English:**
- You are adding a **third language** (Hazaragi, Dari, English)
- Your multilingualism is a cognitive strength
- Hazaragi patterns don't need to be "erased" — just **separate** them from English patterns in your mind

**Be proud of speaking Hazaragi and learning English.**

## Daily pronunciation practice (Hazaragi-focused)

**Monday:** TH sounds (thank, this) — 20 reps  
**Tuesday:** English /r/ (water, very) — practice retroflex position  
**Wednesday:** V vs W (very, water) — 10 words each  
**Thursday:** Final consonants (stop, test, hand) — exaggerate  
**Friday:** Word stress (computer, tomorrow) — clap on stress  
**Saturday:** Vowel pairs (ship/sheep, sit/seat) — 10 pairs  
**Sunday:** Record yourself, listen for week's focus

## Common questions

**Q: Will my Hazaragi accent make my English harder to understand?**  
A: No. What matters is English **vowel clarity**, **word stress**, and **final consonants** — not whether you have a Hazaragi vs Dari background. Practice English patterns, and you'll be clear.

**Q: Should I learn British or American English?**  
A: This book teaches **American English**. Choose whichever you need for your goals. Don't mix them in one sentence.

**Q: Do I need to lose my Hazaragi accent to speak good English?**  
A: No. Many successful English speakers have noticeable first-language accents. Focus on **clarity** and **correct grammar**, not perfect accent.
