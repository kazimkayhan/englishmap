# Word Stress

**Learning goal:** Put stress on the right syllable to be understood

---

## Why word stress matters MORE than perfect sounds

**Word stress is one of the most important aspects of English pronunciation.**

Wrong stress = misunderstanding, even if all your sounds are correct.

**Example:**
- **PRE**-sent (noun — a gift) vs pre-**SENT** (verb — to show)
- **RE**-cord (noun — recording) vs re-**CORD** (verb — to record)

> **Critical fact:** Native speakers understand you better with **correct stress + imperfect sounds** than **perfect sounds + wrong stress**.

---

## What is word stress?

**Stress** means one syllable in a word is:
- **Louder**
- **Longer**
- **Higher pitch**
- More clear vowel

**Unstressed** syllables are:
- Softer
- Shorter
- Lower pitch
- Vowels often reduced to "uh" /ə/

---

## How to mark stress

We use **CAPITAL LETTERS** or **bold** to show stress:
- **COM**-pu-ter → COMputer → **com**puter
- pho-**TO**-graph → phoTOgraph
- pho-to-**GRA**-phy → photoGRAphy

**Notice:** When suffix changes, stress shifts!

> **Dari tip:** Dari stress patterns are different from English. English stress can change word meaning and is more pronounced. Practice exaggerating stress until it feels natural.

---

## Common stress patterns

### 1-syllable words

All 1-syllable words are stressed (they only have one syllable!):
- **book**, **eat**, **go**, **stop**, **hand**

---

### 2-syllable words

#### Pattern A: Stress first syllable (MOST 2-syllable nouns)

| Word | Stress pattern | Syllables |
|------|---------------|-----------|
| **stu**dent | STU-dent | ●○ |
| **tea**cher | TEA-cher | ●○ |
| **wa**ter | WA-ter | ●○ |
| **mo**ther | MO-ther | ●○ |
| **sis**ter | SIS-ter | ●○ |

**Rule:** Most 2-syllable **nouns** stress the **first** syllable.

---

#### Pattern B: Stress second syllable (MOST 2-syllable verbs)

| Word | Stress pattern | Syllables |
|------|---------------|-----------|
| be-**gin** | be-GIN | ○● |
| for-**get** | for-GET | ○● |
| re-**peat** | re-PEAT | ○● |
| be-**lieve** | be-LIEVE | ○● |
| de-**cide** | de-CIDE | ○● |

**Rule:** Most 2-syllable **verbs** stress the **second** syllable.

---

### 3-syllable words

#### Pattern A: First syllable stressed

- **FA**-mi-ly → FAmily
- **E**-ve-ry → Every
- **yes**-ter-day → YEsterday
- **DIF**-fi-cult → DIFficult

---

#### Pattern B: Second syllable stressed

- to-**MOR**-row → toMORrow
- im-**POR**-tant → imPORtant
- com-**PU**-ter → comPUter
- re-**MEM**-ber → reMEMber

---

#### Pattern C: Last syllable stressed (less common)

- un-der-**STAND** → underSTAND
- Ja-pa-**NESE** → JapaNESE
- re-fu-**GEE** → refuGEE

---

### Word families: Stress shifts

**Watch out!** When you add a suffix, stress can move:

| Base word | Stressed syllable | With suffix | New stress |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------|------------|
| **PHO**-to | 1st | pho-**TO**-gra-phy | 2nd |
| **PHO**-to-graph | 1st | pho-to-**GRA**-phic | 3rd |
| e-**DU**-cate | 2nd | e-du-**CA**-tion | 3rd |
| **COM**-mu-ni-cate | 1st | com-mu-ni-**CA**-tion | 4th |

**This is difficult!** Learn stress when you learn the word.

---

## Stress patterns by word ending (suffix rules)

### Stress on syllable BEFORE suffix:

| Suffix | Example | Stress pattern |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| -tion | edu-**CA**-tion, in-for-**MA**-tion | Stress syllable before -tion |
| -sion | de-**CI**-sion, vi-**SION** | Stress syllable before -sion |
| -ic | photo-**GRAPH**-ic, sci-en-**TIF**-ic | Stress syllable before -ic |
| -ity | u-ni-ver-**SI**-ty, sim-pli-**CI**-ty | Stress syllable before -ity |
| -graphy | pho-**TO**-gra-phy, ge-**O**-gra-phy | Stress syllable before -graphy |

---

### Stress on the suffix itself:

| Suffix | Example | Stress |
|--------|---------|--------|
| -ee | em-ploy-**EE**, ref-u-**GEE** | Stress -ee |
| -eer | en-gin-**EER**, vol-un-**TEER** | Stress -eer |
| -ese | Jap-a-**NESE**, Chi-**NESE** | Stress -ese |
| -ique | u-**NIQUE**, tech-**NIQUE** | Stress -ique |

---

## Compound words (two words together)

**Rule:** Usually stress the **first** word:

| Compound | Stress | Example sentence |
|----------|--------|------------------|
| **SMART**-phone | 1st word | I use my **smart**phone. |
| **NOTE**-book | 1st word | I write in my **note**book. |
| **HOME**-work | 1st word | I finish my **home**work. |
| **WEEK**-end | 1st word | I rest on the **week**end. |

**Exception:** Some compounds stress the second word (less common):
- down-**STAIRS**, good-**BYE**

---

## Sentence stress

In sentences, **content words** are stressed:

**Content words (stressed):**
- Nouns: **book**, **student**, **home**
- Main verbs: **study**, **eat**, **work**
- Adjectives: **good**, **happy**, **big**
- Adverbs: **quickly**, **well**, **always**
- Question words: **what**, **where**, **why**
- Negative: **not**, **no**, **never**

**Function words (unstressed):**
- Articles: a, an, the
- Pronouns: I, you, he, she
- Prepositions: in, on, at, to
- Conjunctions: and, but, or
- Auxiliary verbs: am, is, are, do, does

**Example sentence:**
"I **STUDY** **ENGLISH** at **HOME** every **DAY**."

Not: "**I** study **English** **at** home **every** day." (too many stresses)

## Common stress mistakes

### Mistake 1: Equal stress on all syllables

❌ COMP-U-TER (all loud)  
✅ **COM**-pu-ter (first loud, others soft)

**Fix:** Exaggerate stress on main syllable; make others very soft.

---

### Mistake 2: Stressing the wrong syllable

❌ stu-**DENT** (sounds strange)  
✅ **STU**-dent

❌ **TO**-mor-row  
✅ to-**MOR**-row

**Fix:** Look up stress in dictionary or listen to audio. Mark stress when you learn a new word.

---

### Mistake 3: Not reducing unstressed syllables

In English, unstressed vowels often become "uh" /ə/:
- **COM**-pu-ter → COMputuh (not COMpooter)
- to-**MOR**-row → tuhMORrow (not toMORrow)

**This is advanced — focus on strong stress first, vowel reduction later.**

---

## How to practice stress

### Method 1: Clap or tap

- Say the word
- Clap on the stressed syllable
- **CLAP**-pu-ter, to-**CLAP**-row

### Method 2: Exaggerate

- Make stressed syllable **VERY LOUD**
- Make unstressed syllables **very soft**
- **COM**puterrrr (hold the stressed vowel longer)

### Method 3: Mark new words

When you learn a new word, write:
```
tomorrow: to-MOR-row (●○●)
```

●= stressed, ○ = unstressed

### Method 4: Record and listen

- Record yourself saying 10 words
- Listen: Is one syllable clearly stronger?
- Compare to online dictionary audio (e.g., dictionary.com)

## Daily practice (5 minutes)

**Day 1:** 2-syllable nouns (STUdent, TEAcher, WAter) — 20 words  
**Day 2:** 2-syllable verbs (beGIN, forGET, rePEAT) — 20 words  
**Day 3:** 3-syllable words (FAMily, imPORtant, underSTAND) — 15 words  
**Day 4:** Compound words (SMARTphone, HOMEwork, NOTEbook) — 10 words  
**Day 5:** Sentence stress (stress content words only) — 10 sentences  
**Day 6:** Record yourself reading a paragraph; listen for stress  
**Day 7:** Review mistakes from the week
