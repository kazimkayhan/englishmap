EnglishMap — English for Dari & Hazaragi Speakers

Words 701-1000 (Self-Fill Template)

Learning goal: Reach 1000+ active vocabulary words

Why words 701-1000 matter

Reaching 1000 words is a major milestone:

  • You can understand about 80-85% of everyday English
  • You can hold conversations on most common topics
  • You can read intermediate-level texts
  • You're ready for more advanced grammar and writing

These final 300 words should be chosen based on your interests and goals:

  • Technology & internet
  • Health & medical
  • Education & career
  • Hobbies & interests
  • Current events

Important: At this level, you choose which words to learn. Focus on words you actually need and will use.

How this level works

Words 1-300: Essential foundation — everyone needs these
Words 301-700: High-frequency — most learners need these
Words 701-1000: Personalized — you choose based on your goals

Finding your words 701-1000

Strategy 1: Learn from what you read

When you read English articles, stories, or lessons:

  1. Highlight unknown words
  2. Look up the meaning
  3. If the word appears 3+ times in different texts → add it to your list
  4. Write it in your vocabulary notebook

This is the most natural way to build vocabulary at this level.

Choose 3-5 themes that matter to you, then learn 50-100 words per theme.

Strategy 3: Academic word list

If you plan to:

  • Take online courses in English
  • Apply for scholarships
  • Read academic materials

Learn from the Academic Word List (AWL) — 570 important words used in higher education:

  • analyze, approach, area, assess, assume, authority, available, benefit, concept, consist, context, contract, create, data, define, derive, distribute, economy, environment, establish, estimate, evidence, factor, finance, formula, function, identify, income, indicate, individual, interpret, involve, issue, labor, legal, legislate, major, method, occur, percent, period, policy, principle, procedure, process, require, research, respond, role, section, sector, significant, similar, source, specific, structure, theory, vary

These words are essential for academic success.

Strategy 4: Professional vocabulary

Choose words for your field or career goal:

  • Remote work: freelance, client, project, deadline, invoice, payment, platform, portfolio, proposal, contract
  • Teaching: curriculum, assessment, feedback, evaluation, pedagogy, methodology
  • Healthcare: diagnosis, prescription, treatment, prevention, immunization
  • Business: strategy, marketing, customer, product, service, profit, revenue, expense

Your personal vocabulary log (template)

Create a document or note called "My 701-1000 Words" with this format:

### Word 701: [English word]
- Dari: [meaning]
- Category: [technology / health / education / etc.]
- Example: [sentence from where you found it]
- My sentence: [your own sentence]
- Date learned: [date]

### Word 702: [English word]
...

Goal: Add 2-5 words per day from your reading and interests.

How to learn words at this level

1. Learn in context (always)

Don't just memorize word lists. Learn words from:

  • Articles you read
  • Videos you watch (with subtitles)
  • Conversations you hear

2. Focus on collocations

At this level, learn how words work together:

  • strong coffee (not "powerful coffee")
  • make a decision (not "do a decision")
  • take a test (not "make a test")
  • do homework (not "make homework")

3. Learn word families

When you learn a new word, learn its family:

  • educate (verb) → education (noun) → educational (adjective) → educator (noun)
  • communicate (verb) → communication (noun) → communicative (adjective)

4. Use new words within 24 hours

  • Write a sentence
  • Say it aloud
  • Use it in a message or conversation (if safe)

Self-assessment

After learning 100 words at this level:

Can you:

  1. Write 30 words from memory?
  2. Use 20 words in sentences without looking?
  3. Explain 10 words in simple English?
  4. Recognize them when reading?
  5. Use them in speaking or writing naturally?

If yes → you're progressing well.
If no → slow down, focus on fewer words, use them more.

Resources for finding words 701-1000

Simple English websites

  • Simple English Wikipedia (simple.wikipedia.org)
  • VOA Learning English (learningenglish.voanews.com)
  • BBC Learning English (bbclearningenglish.com)

Word frequency tools

  • Use online frequency lists (search "1000 most common English words")
  • Focus on words ranked 701-1000

From your interests

  • If you like technology → read simple tech articles, note new words
  • If you like health → read health tips in English
  • If you like stories → read graded readers (A2-B1 level)

Common mistakes at this level

MistakeFix
Learning words you'll never useChoose words from your actual reading and interests
Only passive recognitionForce yourself to use new words in writing/speaking
Ignoring collocationsLearn phrases, not just single words
Translating too literallySome words don't translate directly — learn meaning from context

Milestone: 1000 words

Congratulations! When you reach 1000 active words:

  • You're at solid A2/B1 level vocabulary
  • You can handle most daily conversations
  • You can read intermediate texts with a dictionary
  • You're ready for more complex grammar and writing

What "active" means:

  • You can use the word in a sentence
  • You can spell it
  • You know common collocations
  • You recognize it when reading or listening

Don't aim for perfect pronunciation or 100% accuracy — aim for confident use.

Next steps

After reaching 1000 words:

  1. Stop counting — vocabulary learning becomes natural from reading and listening
  2. Learn words in context from materials at your level
  3. Focus on using vocabulary in Writing and Speaking
  4. Review Memory Techniques for long-term retention
  5. Move to Intermediate Growth for advanced grammar

Maintenance: Don't forget words 1-700

Weekly review:

  • Monday: Review 50 words from 1-300
  • Wednesday: Review 50 words from 301-700
  • Friday: Review 50 words from 701-1000

Monthly challenge: Write a 200-word essay using words from all three levels.
