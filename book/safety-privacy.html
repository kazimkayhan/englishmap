Safety & Privacy Guide

For young women studying English at home in Afghanistan

Learning English is your right. This guide helps you study safely, privately, and without unnecessary risk.

Physical Safety

Choosing Your Study Space

Safe spaces:

  • ✅ Private room with a door you can close
  • ✅ Quiet corner where you won't be interrupted
  • ✅ Shared space during times when you feel comfortable
  • ✅ Any place where you can focus without fear

Tips:

  • Use headphones for listening practice
  • Keep your study materials in a discrete folder or app
  • If questioned, simple answers work: "I'm learning a skill" or "I'm studying for my future"
  • You don't owe detailed explanations to anyone

Recording Yourself (Speaking Practice)

Voice recording is essential for pronunciation practice, but be cautious:

Safe practices:

  • ✅ Use voice memos app with a strong passcode/lock
  • ✅ Store recordings in a private folder
  • ✅ Delete old recordings after reviewing (weekly cleanup)
  • ✅ Never upload recordings to public platforms
  • ✅ Avoid saying your full name or location in recordings

What to record:

  • Practice sentences: "Hello. I study English."
  • Reading passages from this book
  • Describing simple topics: "My daily routine" (generic)

What NOT to record:

  • Personal identifying information
  • Political opinions or sensitive topics
  • Your location or family details

Digital Safety

Device & App Security

Essential protections:

  1. Strong passcode on your phone/tablet (not 0000 or 1234)
  2. Biometric lock (fingerprint/face) if available
  3. Private browsing mode when using shared devices
  4. Clear browser history after study sessions on shared devices

Learning Apps & Websites

Trusted sources (free & safe):

  • This book (offline mdBook)
  • BBC Learning English (reputable)
  • Duolingo (privacy-conscious)
  • YouTube channels from known institutions (no account needed)

Red flags (avoid):

  • Apps requesting excessive permissions (contacts, camera, microphone for a vocabulary app?)
  • "Free trials" requiring credit card info
  • Apps from unknown developers
  • Platforms asking for real name, photo, and location for basic use

Account Safety

When creating accounts:

  • Use a username, not your real name
  • Create a separate email for learning apps (e.g., Gmail with pseudonym)
  • Strong, unique passwords (write them in a hidden notebook or password manager)
  • Skip optional fields (real name, phone, photo)
  • Never share personal information in profile or bio

Two-factor authentication (2FA):

  • Enable it if the platform offers it
  • Use authenticator apps (Google Authenticator, Authy) rather than SMS if possible
  • Protects your account even if password is guessed

Language Exchange & Online Practice

Safer Options

  • ✅ Text-based platforms (no video/voice)
  • ✅ Apps with reporting and blocking features
  • ✅ Group learning communities (not 1-on-1 chats)
  • ✅ Platforms with moderation (Hello Talk, Tandem have reports/blocks)

What NOT to Share

  • ❌ Real name or nickname others can trace
  • ❌ Location (city, neighborhood, school, landmarks)
  • ❌ Photos of yourself or family
  • ❌ Phone number or personal social media
  • ❌ Daily schedule or routines
  • ❌ Political or religious views (even if asked)

Red Flags — End Conversation Immediately If:

  • Someone asks where you live (city/country is max)
  • Requests move from app to WhatsApp/Telegram right away
  • Asks for photos or video calls
  • Sends inappropriate messages
  • Pressures you to meet in person
  • Shows too much interest in your personal life
  • Makes you feel uncomfortable in any way

Action: Block, report, and move on. You owe no explanation.

Emotional Safety

Managing Shame & Guilt

Remember:

  • Learning is not forbidden by any religion — all major faiths encourage education
  • You have the right to learn and grow
  • Taking breaks is normal, not failure
  • Missing days doesn't erase your progress
  • You are not "behind" — you are on your own path

Dealing with Discouragement

When you feel:

  • "I'm not smart enough" → False. You can learn any skill with practice.
  • "It's too late for me" → False. Adults learn languages successfully every day.
  • "I'll never be fluent" → Fluency is a journey, not a destination. Progress counts.
  • "I've forgotten everything after a break" → Review is faster than learning. You'll catch up quickly.

Action:

  • Read Building Confidence
  • Review your Progress Tracker — see how far you've come
  • Restart with 10 minutes today — that's enough

Comparison & Social Media

Avoid:

  • Comparing yourself to fluent speakers online (they have different histories)
  • Following "polyglot influencers" who make you feel bad
  • Perfectionism — "If I can't study 2 hours, I won't study at all" (wrong mindset)

Practice:

  • Compare yourself to yesterday's you
  • Celebrate small wins: learned 5 words, spoke 3 sentences, studied 15 minutes
  • Unfollow accounts that make you feel inadequate

Privacy in This Book

What This Book Does NOT Do

  • ❌ No tracking or analytics
  • ❌ No account required
  • ❌ No data collection
  • ❌ No internet needed after initial load
  • ❌ No ads or third-party scripts

What This Book DOES

  • ✅ Works completely offline
  • ✅ Stored locally on your device
  • ✅ No record of what you read or when
  • ✅ Private search (nothing leaves your device)

Keeping This Book Private

  • Bookmark as "Study Notes" or a generic name
  • Add to home screen with a discrete icon (mobile)
  • Use private/incognito browser if sharing device
  • Clear cache/history on shared devices

Emergency Protocol

If Someone Finds Your Study Materials

Stay calm. Options:

  1. Minimize:

    • "It's just English vocabulary for reading."
    • "I'm learning to read instructions better."
    • "It's for work/online tasks someday."

  2. Redirect:

    • Change subject
    • Offer to help them with something
    • Close the app calmly (don't panic)

  3. Hide in Plain Sight:

    • Keep book in a folder named "Recipes" or "Notes"
    • Mix study files with other documents
    • Use apps that look like games or utilities

If You Feel Unsafe

  • Stop studying temporarily — your safety comes first
  • Delete apps if needed (you can reinstall later)
  • Study in shorter bursts when alone
  • Consider less obvious study materials (e.g., listening looks like music, reading looks like browsing)

Resources for Safety

If you need help with digital safety or privacy:

  • Access Now (digital security hotline)
  • Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF) guides
  • Internews (safety resources for women)

Long-Term Privacy Habits

1. Regular Cleanups

  • Delete old voice recordings weekly
  • Clear browser history on shared devices
  • Remove apps you no longer use
  • Check app permissions quarterly

2. Separate Identities

  • Learning account ≠ personal account
  • Different passwords for different purposes
  • Use pseudonyms consistently

3. Trust Your Instincts

  • If something feels wrong, stop
  • If someone makes you uncomfortable, block them
  • If an app feels sketchy, delete it
  • Your gut is usually right

Specific Safety Scenarios

Scenario 1: Shared Family Phone

Strategy:

  • Study in private browsing mode
  • Clear history after each session
  • Use apps that can be hidden or password-protected
  • Alternatively, use this book offline without leaving traces

Scenario 2: Limited Privacy at Home

Strategy:

  • Study during times when you're alone
  • Use headphones always (looks like listening to music)
  • Keep screen angled away from doors/windows
  • Have a cover activity ready to switch to (recipes, news, games)

Scenario 3: Public Wi-Fi

Strategy:

  • Download lessons when on home Wi-Fi
  • Use offline mode in public
  • Avoid logging into accounts on public networks
  • Use VPN if available and legal in your area

Balancing Safety & Learning

Your education matters. Your safety matters more.

If you must choose between:

  • Studying openly but at risk → Choose safety, study discreetly
  • Perfect practice vs. safe practice → Choose safe
  • Recording yourself vs. staying silent → Silent practice is still practice

Alternative low-profile study methods:

  • Read silently (still effective)
  • Write vocabulary in a regular notebook (looks like any notes)
  • Listen to English as "music" with headphones
  • Practice pronunciation silently (mouth movements only)

Remember

  • You have the right to learn — this is not wrong or shameful
  • Small risks for big rewards — but assess them honestly
  • Privacy is protection — use it wisely
  • You are not alone — millions of women study discreetly worldwide
  • Breaks for safety are not failure — come back when you can
