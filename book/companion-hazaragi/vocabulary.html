Hazaragi-English Sentence Patterns

Present Tense

Hazaragi:

Man engelīsī mī-khōnam.

Word-for-word:

"I English [present marker]-read."

English:

"I study/read English."

Past Tense

Hazaragi:

Man dērōz ketow khāndom.

Word-for-word:

"I yesterday book read-[past]."

English:

"I read a book yesterday."

Future (using khāham / "I will")

Hazaragi:

Man fardā be maktab khāham raft.

Word-for-word:

"I tomorrow to school will go-[past participle]."

English:

"I will go to school tomorrow."

Study Tips: Hazaragi → English

  1. Don't translate word-for-word

    • Hazaragi: SOV (Subject-Object-Verb)
    • English: SVO (Subject-Verb-Object)
    • Example: "I book read" → "I read a book"

  2. Add articles (a, an, the)

    • Hazaragi has no articles
    • English requires them: "I have book" → "I have a book"

  3. English needs pronouns as subjects

    • Hazaragi: Mī-khōnam (verb alone, "I read/am reading")
    • English: I read (subject required)

  4. Hazaragi plurals are flexible; English plurals are strict

    • Hazaragi: mardim can mean "people" (already plural)
    • English: man → men, woman → women (irregular)

Regional Variations Note

Hazaragi varies across:

  • Hazarajat (Bamyan, Daikundi, Ghazni, etc.) — Central Afghan Hazaragi
  • Quetta (Pakistan) — Quetta Hazaragi has Urdu/Pashto influence
  • Iran — Iranian Hazaragi has more Persian influence
  • Diaspora — Mixed features

This guide uses Central Afghan Hazaragi as the baseline. If your words differ, they're still valid!

Practice Exercises

Exercise 1: Translate to English

  1. Man dar kor-am engelīsī mī-khōnam.
  2. Mār-am ketow mī-khōnad.
  3. Aftow besyār garm ast.

Answers:

  1. I study English at home.
  2. My mother reads a book.
  3. The sun is very hot.

Exercise 2: Translate to Hazaragi (try first, then check)

  1. I drink water every day.
  2. My brother goes to school.
  3. She is tired.

Suggested Hazaragi:

  1. Man har rōz āw mī-nōsham.
  2. Barār-am be maktab mī-berem.
  3. Ow khasta ast. (ow = she/he)
