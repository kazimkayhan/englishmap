EnglishMap — English for Dari & Hazaragi Speakers

Vowel Sounds

Learning goal: Hear and produce American English vowel sounds clearly

Why vowels matter

Vowels are the heart of every syllable. Getting vowels wrong makes words hard to understand:

  • "ship" vs "sheep" (different vowels, different meanings)
  • "sit" vs "seat"
  • "cat" vs "cut"

English has more vowel sounds than Dari, so this takes practice.

Goal: Understood, not perfect. Focus on sounds that cause the most confusion.

Most confusing pairs for Dari speakers

Pair 1: /ɪ/ vs /iː/ (ship vs sheep)

/ɪ/ short/iː/ long
shipsheep
live (verb)leave
iteat
bitbeat
sitseat

Practice sentence:
"I live here, but I will leave tomorrow."

How to fix:

  • /ɪ/: Short, quick, relaxed
  • /iː/: Long, tense, hold 2x longer

Pair 2: /ɛ/ vs /æ/ (bed vs bad)

/ɛ//æ/
bedbad
penpan
setsat
metmat

How to tell difference:

  • /ɛ/: Mouth open medium (like saying "eh")
  • /æ/: Mouth open wider, tongue flat (like saying "aah" at the doctor)

Practice: Look in a mirror. For /æ/, your mouth opens wider.

Pair 3: /ʌ/ vs /ɑː/ (cup vs cop in some accents)

/ʌ//ɑː/
cupcar
butbar
lucklark

How to fix:

  • /ʌ/: Relaxed, middle of mouth (like a small grunt)
  • /ɑː/: Open wide, back of mouth, longer

Dari tip: The sound /ʌ/ doesn't exist in Dari. Practice saying "uh" as in "uh-oh" — that's /ʌ/.

Pair 4: /ʊ/ vs /uː/ (book vs boot)

/ʊ//uː/
bookboot
goodfood
footfruit

How to fix:

  • /ʊ/: Short, less rounded lips
  • /uː/: Long, tightly rounded lips, hold longer

Vowel sounds that don't exist in Dari

Sound 1: /ɜː/ (bird, word, her)

How to make it:

  1. Relax your mouth (not wide, not closed)
  2. Tongue in middle position
  3. Make a sound from your throat: "errr"

Words to practice:
bird, word, her, first, nurse, work, learn, girl, turn, early

Don't replace with /a/ or /e/ sounds.

Sound 2: /æ/ (cat, bad, hand)

How to make it:

  1. Open mouth wider than for /ɛ/
  2. Flatten your tongue
  3. Say "aah" like at the doctor

Words to practice:
cat, bad, hand, family, man, can, back, sad, apple, have

Common mistake: Dari speakers often say /ɛ/ (bed sound) instead.

Daily practice routine (5-10 minutes)

Day 1-3: Focus on /ɪ/ vs /iː/

  • Say: ship-sheep, live-leave, it-eat (10 times each pair)
  • Record yourself
  • Listen and compare

Day 4-6: Focus on /ɛ/ vs /æ/

  • Say: bed-bad, pen-pan, set-sat (10 times each)
  • Exaggerate mouth opening for /æ/
  • Check in mirror

Day 7-9: Focus on /ʌ/

  • Say: cup, but, love, come, some (20 times)
  • Think: "uh" as in "uh-oh"

Day 10-12: Focus on /ɜː/

  • Say: bird, word, her, first, learn (20 times)
  • Relax mouth, middle tongue position

Day 13-14: Mix all sounds

  • Read 10 sentences with all vowel types
  • Record and listen

Vowel practice sentences

Sentence 1 (short /ɪ/):

"It is difficult to sit still."

Sentence 2 (long /iː/):

"I need to sleep and eat."

Sentence 3 (/æ/):

"The cat sat on a black mat."

Sentence 4 (/ɜː/):

"The girl learned her first words early."

Sentence 5 (mixed):

"I live in a big city. I leave at six. I eat good food."

Self-check quiz

  1. Which vowel is longer: "ship" or "sheep"? (sheep)
  2. Does "cat" have /ɛ/ or /æ/? (/æ/ — wider mouth)
  3. Say "book" and "boot" — which has rounded lips? (both, but "boot" is more rounded and longer)
  4. What sound is in "bird" and "word"? (/ɜː/)
  5. Record yourself saying "live" (verb) and "leave" — can you hear the difference?

Common mistakes

MistakeExampleFix
All short vowels sound the same"sit" sounds like "set"Practice /ɪ/ vs /ɛ/ with mirror
Replacing /ɜː/ with /a/"bird" → "bard"Practice "errr" sound
No difference between /ɪ/ and /iː/"ship" = "sheep"Hold /iː/ 2x longer, tense your tongue
/æ/ too close to /ɛ/"cat" sounds like "cat"Open mouth wider for /æ/

Tips for improvement

  1. Use a mirror: Watch your mouth shape
  2. Record yourself: Compare to American English audio
  3. Exaggerate first: Make the difference HUGE, then make it natural
  4. One sound per week: Don't try to fix everything at once
  5. Forgive yourself: Accent is normal; clarity is the goal

Hazaragi tip: If you're used to Hazaragi vowel sounds, some might be closer to English than Dari vowels are. Listen carefully to which sounds are easier for you.
