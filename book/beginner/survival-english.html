EnglishMap — English for Dari & Hazaragi Speakers

Survival English

Learning goal: Use essential phrases for daily situations

What is survival English?

Survival English is the minimum language you need to:

  • Greet people politely
  • Ask for help
  • Say thank you
  • Handle simple conversations
  • Express basic needs

These phrases work even if your grammar isn't perfect yet. Politeness and trying are more important than perfection.

Real-world goal: After this lesson, you can introduce yourself to an English speaker and ask for basic help.

Real-life scenarios

Scenario 1: Meeting someone for the first time

Person A: Hello.
You: Hi. My name is Fatima.
Person A: Nice to meet you, Fatima. I'm Anna.
You: Nice to meet you too, Anna.
Person A: How are you?
You: I'm fine, thank you. And you?
Person A: I'm good, thanks.

Scenario 2: Asking for help

You: Excuse me. I need help, please.
Helper: Of course. What do you need?
You: I don't understand this message. Can you speak slowly?
Helper: Sure. (explains slowly)
You: Thank you very much.
Helper: You're welcome.

Scenario 3: Online chat (written)

Friend: Hey! How are you?
You: Hi! I'm fine, thank you. How are you?
Friend: Good! Are you busy?
You: No, I have time now.
Friend: Great. I need to ask you something.
You: Sure. What is it?

Scenario 4: Saying you don't understand

Speaker: (says something fast)
You: I'm sorry, I don't understand. Can you repeat that slowly, please?
Speaker: (repeats slowly)
You: Thank you. I understand now.

Scenario 5: Expressing needs

You: Excuse me. I need water, please.
Person: Sure, one moment.
(Person brings water)
You: Thank you very much.
Person: You're welcome.

Phone typing practice

Practice typing these common texts on your phone:

  1. "Hello. How are you?"
  2. "Thank you for your help."
  3. "I don't understand. Can you help me, please?"
  4. "My name is [your name]. Nice to meet you."
  5. "I'm fine, thank you."

Cultural notes

Politeness in American English

  • "Please" and "thank you" are used frequently
  • "Excuse me" gets someone's attention politely
  • It's okay to say "I don't know" — honesty is valued
  • Saying "I'm sorry" when you don't understand is polite, even if it's not your fault

Direct vs indirect

  • English (especially American English) is often more direct than Dari
  • It's normal to say "I don't understand" directly
  • Asking "Can you help me?" is not rude — it's expected

Hazaragi note: Hazaragi speakers often use more indirect requests. In English, especially for survival situations, being direct and clear is better: "I need help" is clearer than "Maybe if you have time..."

Practice activities

Activity 1: Self-introduction video

Record a 30-second voice memo:

  • Say hello
  • Give your name
  • Say "Nice to meet you"
  • Say "Thank you" and "Goodbye"

Activity 2: Write 5 help requests

Write five different ways to ask for help using the phrases above.

Activity 3: Daily greeting routine

Every morning, say aloud (to yourself): "Good morning. My name is ____. Today is a good day."

Activity 4: Type survival messages

Open your notes app and type:

  • 3 greetings
  • 3 thank you messages
  • 3 help requests

Self-check quiz

  1. How do you say سلام in English? (Hello / Hi)
  2. What do you say when someone helps you? (Thank you)
  3. Fill in: "I ____ understand." (for expressing confusion) (don't)
  4. What phrase do you use to get someone's attention politely? (Excuse me)
  5. Respond to "How are you?" ("I'm fine, thank you.")

Memory tips

The "Hello Chain" (memorize as one flow)

"Hello. My name is ____. Nice to meet you. How are you? I'm fine, thank you."

The "Help Chain"

"Excuse me. I need help, please. I don't understand. Can you speak slowly? Thank you."

Practice out loud daily

  • Morning: Hello chain
  • Evening: Help chain
  • Before bed: Say "Goodbye. See you tomorrow."
