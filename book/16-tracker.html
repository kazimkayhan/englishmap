EnglishMap — English for Dari & Hazaragi Speakers

Progress Tracker

Track small wins. Numbers are proof that you are moving forward.

Learning goals

By using this tracker weekly, you will:

  1. Build a daily study habit (even 15 minutes counts)
  2. See which skills you practice most and least
  3. Set realistic monthly goals
  4. Recover motivation after breaks
  5. Prepare for checkpoint quizzes in 14-assessments.md

Why tracking matters

When life is hard, memory lies. You forget that you learned 200 words last month. A tracker shows the truth: you are growing.

What gets measured gets improved — but kindness matters more than perfection.

Level focus

All stages (0–4). Update checklists as you advance.

Step-by-step: set up your tracker (15 minutes)

Lesson 1 — Choose your tool

Pick one:

  • Phone Notes app (simplest)
  • Paper notebook (no battery needed)
  • Copy assets/tracker-template.md into Notes

Lesson 2 — Baseline (Day 1)

Write today’s date and answer:

QuestionYour answer
Can I read the English alphabet?yes / some / no
Can I say hello and thank you?yes / no
Minutes I can study per day15 / 30 / 45
My main reason to learn English_____________

Lesson 3 — Weekly rhythm

Every Sunday (or your chosen day):

  1. Count study days (goal: 5–7)
  2. Total minutes
  3. One skill to improve next week
  4. Copy a new week from the template

Lesson 4 — Monthly checkpoint (30 minutes)

On the last day of each month:

  1. Complete the Stage checklist below
  2. Record yourself speaking 60 seconds — compare to last month (optional)
  3. Take the quiz for your level in 14-assessments.md

Stage checklists

Stage 0 — Pre-beginner

  • I know the English alphabet
  • I can type my name on a phone keyboard
  • I know 50 survival words
  • I can say: Hello, Thank you, My name is…

Stage 1 — Beginner (A1–A2)

  • I use present tense for daily habits
  • I know 300+ common words
  • I can write a 5-sentence message about my day
  • I understand slow English (clear audio)
  • I studied 60+ days total (not necessarily in a row)

Stage 2 — Intermediate (B1)

  • I use past tense for stories
  • I know 800+ words
  • I can write one paragraph (80+ words)
  • I understand normal-speed short videos with some repetition
  • I speak 2 minutes without switching to Dari

Stage 3 — Upper intermediate (B2–C1)

  • I use conditionals and complex sentences
  • I read news or blog articles with a dictionary sometimes
  • I write formal emails
  • I understand podcasts with focus

Stage 4 — Advanced (C1–C2)

  • I argue a position politely in English
  • I write clear multi-paragraph essays
  • I adjust tone (formal vs friendly)
  • I learn new topics primarily through English

Practice tasks

  1. Set up tracker today (Lesson 1).
  2. Log every study day for 7 days — even 15 minutes.
  3. Rate yourself on the skills scorecard.
  4. Complete Stage 0 or Stage 1 checklist honestly.

Self-check questions

  1. Why is tracking helpful when you feel “I learned nothing”?
  2. What is a good minimum on a busy day?
  3. How often should you do a monthly checkpoint?
  4. Should you delete old tracker pages? (Hint: keep them to see growth.)

Study habits (routine card)

When: Same time daily if possible (morning or after chores)
Where: Safe, quiet corner
How long: 30–45 min, or 15 min minimum
After study: One sentence in tracker: “Today I won by…”
After break: Open tracker → read last win → study 15 min
