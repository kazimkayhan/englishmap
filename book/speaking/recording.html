EnglishMap — English for Dari & Hazaragi Speakers

Self-Recording Practice

Learning goal: Use your phone to track speaking progress

Why record yourself?

Your ears hear yourself differently when you speak vs when you listen to a recording. Recording lets you:

  • Hear your real pronunciation
  • Track improvement over time
  • Identify specific problems
  • Build confidence through visible progress

Fact: Most learners are surprised their English sounds better than they thought!

How to record safely (privacy first)

Storage tips

  • Create a private folder: "English Practice" or similar
  • Password-protect if your phone has folder locks
  • Use private/encrypted voice memo apps if available
  • Delete old recordings monthly (or keep best ones only)

Sharing

  • Do NOT post publicly unless you explicitly want to
  • If sharing with a study partner: use secure messaging (Signal, WhatsApp)
  • If using language exchange apps: check app privacy settings

What to record

Week 1: Introduction (30 seconds)

"Hello. My name is [name]. I am learning English. I study at home every day. I want to improve my speaking."

Week 2-4: Daily routine (1 minute)

Describe your morning, afternoon, or evening in simple sentences.

Month 2-3: Topics (2 minutes)

  • My family
  • My goals
  • Why I learn English
  • A memory
  • My favorite book/show

Month 4+: Advanced (3-5 minutes)

  • Explain a concept
  • Give your opinion with reasons
  • Tell a story
  • Describe a process

Recording routine (5-10 minutes)

Step 1: Choose a topic or sentence to practice
Step 2: Practice saying it 2-3 times (no recording yet)
Step 3: Record
Step 4: Listen once
Step 5: Note ONE thing done well + ONE thing to improve
Step 6: Re-record (optional) if you want
Step 7: Save or delete based on your privacy comfort

What to listen for

1. Clarity

  • Can you understand your own words?
  • Are final consonants clear? (stop, test, hand)

2. Rhythm and stress

  • Do content words stand out? (I STUDY ENGLISH DAILY)
  • Or do all words sound equally loud? (needs work)

3. Specific sounds

  • TH sounds (thank, this)
  • V vs W (very, water)
  • Vowel length (ship vs sheep)

4. Pace

  • Too fast? (hard to understand)
  • Too slow? (unnatural pauses)
  • Just right? (clear with natural pauses)

5. Confidence

  • Do you sound hesitant or confident?
  • Do you pause mid-sentence often?

Don't criticize harshly. Note areas to practice, then celebrate that you tried.

Tracking progress: Before & After

Month 1 recording

  • Save your first 30-second introduction
  • Label it "Month 1 — Intro"

Month 3 recording

  • Record the same introduction again
  • Compare with Month 1
  • Notice improvements (even small ones!)

This is powerfully motivating. You WILL sound better.

Common recording mistakes

MistakeFix
Only recording once, never listeningListen to at least 20% of your recordings
Being too harsh on yourselfNote ONE good thing before noting problems
Never re-recordingIt's okay to try 2-3 times
Recording material too hardStart simple, build complexity
Not dating recordingsLabel with date or month — track progress!

Weekly recording plan

DayTopicLength
MonIntroduction30 sec
WedDaily routine1 min
FriOpinion on one topic1-2 min

Total time per week: 10-15 minutes recording, 5 minutes listening.

Recording for specific goals

Goal: Improve pronunciation

  • Record 10 sentences with problem sounds (TH, V, R)
  • Listen: Did you produce those sounds?
  • Practice the ones you missed
  • Re-record next week

Goal: Build fluency

  • Record yourself speaking for 2 minutes without stopping (even if you make mistakes)
  • Goal = flow, not perfection
  • Track: Can you speak longer without pausing awkwardly?

Goal: Prepare for conversation

  • Record answers to common questions:
    • What is your name?
    • Where are you from?
    • What do you do?
    • Why are you learning English?
  • Practice until answers feel automatic

Apps for recording (optional)

Built-in voice memos (iPhone/Android) work fine. But if you want more features:

  • Voice Recorder apps with playback speed control
  • Note-taking apps with voice recording (Evernote, OneNote)
  • Language learning apps with recording feature (some have feedback)

Privacy reminder: Read app privacy policies before using.

Advanced: Transcribe your own recording

Challenge (Month 3+):

  1. Record yourself speaking for 1 minute
  2. Listen and write down EXACTLY what you said (dictation of yourself!)
  3. Check grammar, spelling, word choice
  4. Notice patterns: Do you always use simple words? Do you repeat "and" too much?

This reveals your speaking patterns.

Recording + other skills

Recording + Shadowing

  • Shadow a sentence from audio
  • Record yourself shadowing
  • Compare your version to the original
  • Goal: Match rhythm and stress

Recording + Pronunciation

  • After practicing a specific sound (TH, V, R), record yourself
  • Listen: Did you produce it correctly?

Recording + Writing

  • Write a short paragraph
  • Read it aloud and record
  • Check: Does it sound natural, or too written/formal?

Self-check: Am I using recording effectively?

After 4 weeks:

  1. Do I record at least once per week? ✅ / ❌
  2. Do I listen to my recordings? ✅ / ❌
  3. Do I note improvements? ✅ / ❌
  4. Do I store recordings safely? ✅ / ❌
  5. Do I feel more confident hearing my own voice? ✅ / ❌

If mostly ✅ → keep going, increase recording frequency
If mostly ❌ → start with once a week, listen to every recording

Encouragement

Hearing your own voice feels weird at first. Everyone experiences this, even native speakers.

Week 1: "Do I really sound like that?"
Week 2: "This is uncomfortable."
Week 3: "I'm getting used to it."
Week 4: "I can hear improvement!"

The discomfort fades. The progress stays.
