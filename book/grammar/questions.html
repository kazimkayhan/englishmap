EnglishMap — English for Dari & Hazaragi Speakers

Questions & Negatives

Learning goal: Ask clear questions and make negative statements correctly

Why questions and negatives matter

Being able to ask questions lets you:

  • Get information you need
  • Clarify when you don't understand
  • Have real conversations

Negative sentences let you:

  • Say what you don't do or don't have
  • Correct mistakes
  • Express preferences

Both are essential for communication beyond basic statements.

Yes/No questions (expect "yes" or "no" answer)

With "be" (am, is, are)

Pattern: Move "be" to the front

StatementQuestionAnswer
You are ready.Are you ready?Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
She is at home.Is she at home?Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.
They are students.Are they students?Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.

With "have" (possession, not "have to")

Pattern: Use Do / Does + subject + have

StatementQuestionAnswer
You have a phone.Do you have a phone?Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
She has a sister.Does she have a sister?Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

Important: Don't say "Have you a phone?" in American English. Use "Do you have a phone?"

With other verbs (present simple)

Pattern: Use Do / Does + subject + base verb

StatementQuestionAnswer
You study English.Do you study English?Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
She works at home.Does she work at home?Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.
They speak Dari.Do they speak Dari?Yes, they do. / No, they don't.

Remember: After "does," use the base form (no -s).

  • ✅ Does she work?
  • ❌ Does she works?

Dari tip: In Dari, you can make a question just by changing your tone. In English, you MUST change the word order: "Do you study?" not "You study?"

Wh- questions (need specific information)

Question words

WordAsks aboutExample
Whatthing or actionWhat do you study?
WhereplaceWhere do you live?
WhentimeWhen do you study?
WhopersonWho is your teacher?
WhyreasonWhy do you learn English?
Howmanner or methodHow do you study?
WhichchoiceWhich book do you like?
WhosepossessionWhose phone is this?
How manyquantity (countable)How many sisters do you have?
How muchquantity (uncountable) or priceHow much time do you have?

Pattern for wh- questions

With "be":

  • Question word + be + subject
  • Where are you?
  • Who is she?
  • Why is he late?

With other verbs:

  • Question word + do/does + subject + base verb
  • What do you study?
  • Where does she work?
  • When do they eat lunch?

Special case: "Who" as subject

When "who" is doing the action (subject), don't use do/does:

  • Who studies English? → Sara studies English.
  • Who lives here? → My family lives here.
  • Who has a phone? → She has a phone.

But when "who" is the object:

  • Who do you help? → I help my sister.
  • Who does she call? → She calls her friend.

"How" questions with adjectives

PatternExampleAsks about
How oldHow old are you?Age
How tallHow tall is he?Height
How longHow long is the class?Duration
How farHow far is Kabul?Distance
How oftenHow often do you study?Frequency

Hazaragi tip: "How" questions in Hazaragi might use different structures. In English, put "How + adjective" at the start: "How old are you?" not "You are how old?"

Negative sentences

With "be"

Pattern: Subject + be + not

StatementNegativeContraction
I am tired.I am not tired.I**'m not** tired.
You are late.You are not late.You aren't late.
She is here.She is not here.She isn't here.
We are ready.We are not ready.We aren't ready.

With "have"

Pattern: Subject + do/does not have

StatementNegativeContraction
I have time.I do not have time.I don't have time.
She has Wi-Fi.She does not have Wi-Fi.She doesn't have Wi-Fi.
They have books.They do not have books.They don't have books.

With other verbs

Pattern: Subject + do/does not + base verb

StatementNegativeContraction
I study at night.I do not study at night.I don't study at night.
She works on Friday.She does not work on Friday.She doesn't work on Friday.
They speak English.They do not speak English.They don't speak English.

Critical rule: After "do not" / "does not," use the base form (no -s).

  • ✅ She doesn't work.
  • ❌ She doesn't works.

Never, rarely, seldom (negative meaning)

These words have negative meaning, so don't use "not" with them:

CorrectWrong
I never study at night.I don't never study. ❌
She rarely calls.She doesn't rarely call. ❌
We seldom go out.We don't seldom go. ❌

Short answers

When someone asks a yes/no question, answer with yes/no + subject + auxiliary.

With "be"

  • Are you tired? → Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
  • Is she home? → Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.

With "do/does"

  • Do you study? → Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
  • Does she work? → Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

Don't repeat the main verb in short answers:

  • ❌ Do you study? → Yes, I study.
  • ✅ Do you study? → Yes, I do.

Question tags (confirmation)

A question tag turns a statement into a question to confirm information.

Pattern: Positive statement → negative tag | Negative statement → positive tag

StatementTagFull sentence
You are ready,aren't you?You are ready, aren't you?
She works here,doesn't she?She works here, doesn't she?
They don't know,do they?They don't know, do they?

Use: Check if something is true, or invite agreement.

Practice activities

Activity 1: Make yes/no questions

  1. You are tired. → Are you tired?
  2. She has a phone. → Does she have a phone?
  3. They study English. → Do they study English?

Activity 2: Make wh- questions

Ask about the bold part:

  1. I live in Kabul. → Where do you live?
  2. She studies in the morning. → When does she study?
  3. I study English. → What do you study?

Activity 3: Make negative

  1. I am busy. → I am not busy.
  2. She works at night. → She doesn't work at night.
  3. They have Wi-Fi. → They don't have Wi-Fi.

Activity 4: Short answers

  1. Do you study English? → Yes, I do.
  2. Is she at home? → No, she isn't.
  3. Does he work? → Yes, he does.

Self-check quiz

  1. Make question: "She is tired." → ___?
  2. Make question: "You study English." → ___?
  3. Ask about time: "I study at night." → ___?
  4. Make negative: "I have time." → ___
  5. Fix error: "Where you live?"

Answers:

  1. Is she tired?
  2. Do you study English?
  3. When do you study?
  4. I don't have time. / I do not have time.
  5. Where do you live?

Polite questions

Add please to make requests more polite:

  • Can you help me, please?
  • Please, can you speak slowly?
  • Do you have time, please?

Use Could instead of Can for extra politeness (covered in intermediate):

  • Could you help me, please? (more polite)

Daily practice plan

Day 1: Write 10 yes/no questions with "be"
Day 2: Write 10 yes/no questions with "do/does"
Day 3: Write 10 wh- questions (mix: what, where, when, why, how)
Day 4: Write 10 negative sentences
Day 5: Practice short answers (write Q&A pairs)
Day 6: Write 5 question tags
Day 7: Have a mock dialogue asking and answering 10 questions
