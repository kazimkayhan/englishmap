EnglishMap — English for Dari & Hazaragi Speakers

Be & Have

Learning goal: Master the two most important verbs in English

Why these verbs matter

Be and have are the foundation of English grammar. You use them in almost every sentence. They work differently from other verbs, so learn them separately first.

Critical fact: In Dari, you can drop "to be" in present tense. In English, you cannot. Always include am/is/are.

Verb "be" (am, is, are)

Forms in present tense

SubjectBe formExample
IamI am tired.
youareYou are kind.
he / she / itisShe is at home.
weareWe are students.
theyareThey are ready.

Three main uses

1. Identity / occupation

  • I am a student.
  • She is a teacher.
  • They are sisters.

2. Description / state

  • I am happy.
  • The water is cold.
  • We are tired.

3. Location

  • I am at home.
  • He is in the room.
  • They are here.

Negative form: add "not"

SubjectNegativeShort formExample
Iam notI'm notI am not busy. / I**'m not** busy.
youare notaren't / you're notYou are not late. / You aren't late.
he/she/itis notisn't / he's notShe is not here. / She isn't here.
weare notaren't / we're notWe are not ready. / We aren't ready.
theyare notaren't / they're notThey are not home. / They aren't home.

Note: "Am not" has no common contraction like "amn't" — use "I'm not."

Question form: swap subject and "be"

StatementQuestionAnswer
You are fine.Are you fine?Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
She is at home.Is she at home?Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.
They are ready.Are they ready?Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.

Wh- questions:

  • Where are you?
  • Who is she?
  • Why are they late?

Dari tip: In Dari questions, you can keep the same word order. In English, you MUST move the verb: "Are you ready?" not "You are ready?"

Common "be" expressions (memorize these)

ExpressionMeaningExample
be carefulpay attentionBe careful with hot water.
be quietdon't make noisePlease be quiet in class.
be patientwait calmlyBe patient with yourself.
be readypreparedI am ready to start.
be surecertainI am sure it's correct.
be sorryapologeticI am sorry I'm late.

Verb "have" (have, has)

Forms in present tense

SubjectFormExample
IhaveI have a phone.
youhaveYou have time.
he / she / ithasShe has a sister.
wehaveWe have Wi-Fi today.
theyhaveThey have books.

Main uses

1. Possession (own)

  • I have a notebook.
  • She has two phones.

2. Relationships

  • I have one sister.
  • He has many friends.

3. Experiences or states

  • I have time today.
  • She has a problem.
  • We have a question.

Negative form: use "do not" or "does not"

SubjectNegativeShort formExample
I / you / we / theydo not havedon't haveI do not have Wi-Fi. / I don't have Wi-Fi.
he / she / itdoes not havedoesn't haveShe does not have time. / She doesn't have time.

Important: Do NOT say "I haven't" for simple possession in American English. Say "I don't have."

Question form: use "do" or "does"

StatementQuestionAnswer
You have a phone.Do you have a phone?Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
She has a sister.Does she have a sister?Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

Wh- questions:

  • What do you have?
  • Who has a notebook?
  • How many sisters do you have?

Common "have" expressions

ExpressionMeaningExample
have timebe availableDo you have time to help?
have a questionneed to askI have a question.
have a problemface difficultyShe has a problem with her phone.
have funenjoyHave fun studying!
have a breakrest periodLet's have a break.
have lunch / teaeat/drinkI have tea every morning.

Be vs Have: Key differences

UseBeHave
IdentityI am a student.(not used)
Possession(not used)I have a book.
AgeI am 22.I am 22 years old. (both work)
DescriptionShe is kind.(not used)
StateI am tired.I have energy. (noun)
LocationI am home.(not used)

Common confusion:

WrongRightWhy
I have 22 years.I am 22. / I am 22 years old.Age uses "be"
I am a phone.I have a phone.Possession uses "have"
She has tired.She is tired."Tired" is adjective, use "be"

Dari note: Dari uses داشتن (dâštan) for both possession and some states. English separates these: "I have a book" (possession) vs "I am tired" (state/adjective).

Practice activities

Activity 1: Fill in "be"

Complete with am/is/are:

  1. I ___ fine.
  2. She ___ my sister.
  3. They ___ at home.
  4. You ___ ready.
  5. We ___ students.

Answers: 1. am | 2. is | 3. are | 4. are | 5. are

Activity 2: Fill in "have"

Complete with have/has:

  1. I ___ a phone.
  2. She ___ two sisters.
  3. We ___ time.
  4. He ___ a question.
  5. They ___ books.

Answers: 1. have | 2. has | 3. have | 4. has | 5. have

Activity 3: Make questions

Turn these into yes/no questions:

  1. You are tired. → Are you tired?
  2. She has a phone. → Does she have a phone?
  3. They are ready. → Are they ready?

Activity 4: Choose "be" or "have"

  1. I ___ happy. (am)
  2. She ___ a book. (has)
  3. We ___ at home. (are)
  4. I ___ time. (have)

Self-check quiz

  1. Complete: "I ___ a student." (am / have)
  2. Complete: "She ___ a sister." (is / has)
  3. Make negative: "I am busy." → "I ___ busy."
  4. Make question: "You are ready." → "___ you ready?"
  5. Fix this error: "I have tired."

Answers: 1. am | 2. has | 3. am not / I'm not | 4. Are | 5. I am tired.

Common mistakes (Dari speakers)

MistakeWhy wrongCorrect
I fine.Missing "am"I am fine.
She have book.Wrong form with "she"She has a book.
I have 20 years.Wrong verb for ageI am 20 (years old).
Are you have phone?Mixed structureDo you have a phone?
I am have book.Two verbsI have a book.

Daily practice (5 minutes)

Monday: Write 10 sentences with "am/is/are"
Tuesday: Write 10 sentences with "have/has"
Wednesday: Turn 5 statements into questions
Thursday: Practice negative forms
Friday: Mix all forms in 1 paragraph about your day
